From source document to approved reader page

The original evidence remains authoritative; every derivative and AI addition has a visible boundary.

1 · Register

Source record

Original URL, citation, version/date, file hash, access date, rights state.

2 · Derive

OCR / Markdown

Searchable text with page anchors, extraction method, quality warnings, and link to the source.

3 · Interpret

Claims and gaps

Claim → exact locator → evidence status. Conflicts and nondisclosure remain visible.

4 · Extend

AI contribution

Gap-closing note or separate hypothesis, with assumptions, source inputs, and falsifier.

5 · Approve

Publish bundle

Human-reviewed narrative, structured records, allowed assets, version, and audit links.

Fail closed, not silently

What blocks or limits publication?

  • Rights unresolved: no local PDF; original link only.
  • OCR unreliable: warning retained; extracted text cannot verify quotations.
  • Locator missing: claim remains draft or unresolved.
  • Sources conflict: preserve both positions and expose the conflict.
  • AI support weak: keep as review draft, not approved gap-closing content.
  • Source changed: retain published version and open a review for the newer one.

Canonical versus derivative

Canonical evidence: the cited source document/version.

Search derivative: OCR or Markdown carrying page/section anchors and quality metadata.

Reader explanation: reviewed narrative linked back to claims and sources.

Automated preflight

Schema checks · missing locators · broken links · rights allowlist · forbidden raw-repo paths · evidence-label consistency · glossary coverage · accessibility checks.
