## Short supporting quotations (from captured abstract/conclusions)
- Purpose: "introduce a modified method for recovering and recycling huge number of cigarette butts (CBs) and convert them into a fashion product."
- Approach: "considering that cellulose acetate is the main component and it is soluble in acetone or ethyl acetate, we propose a methodology to purify this polymer-based on several solid-liquid extraction steps using solvents with different polarity."
- Characterization: "Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) were employed to characterize the structural and thermal properties of purified CA compared to unused CA."
- Result: "We showed that recovered CA has the same chemical, thermal properties of unused CA, the purification method does not alter the material properties." and "we can obtain a transparent film from acetate cellulose recovered by cigarette butts using a facile and green extraction method."
- Cigarette butt composition note: "aromatic and heterocyclic amines, carbonylated compounds, phenols, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, carbon and nitrogen oxides, and ammonia. These compounds show different solubility according to their polarity."

## Disclosed mechanism and process parameters
- Mechanism: solid-liquid extraction (washing/leaching) of cigarette butts using solvents of different polarity (acetone or ethyl acetate named for CA solubility) to purify cellulose acetate, producing a recovered CA film; FTIR + TGA validation vs unused CA; degradation kinetics via Kissinger/Ozawa.
- Parameters disclosed only qualitatively: solvents acetone or ethyl acetate; "several solid-liquid extraction steps"; solvent polarity sequence; FTIR/TGA characterization. No numeric solvent ratios, temperatures, times, number of stages, yields, or purity percentages captured in the scraped text.
