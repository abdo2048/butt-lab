ABSTRACT  
Environmental and health concerns stemming from cigarette butt waste and water contamination by reactive 
dyes underscore the need for sustainable solutions. This study presented an innovative approach that repurposes 
discarded cigarette butts as a source of cellulose fibers (CFs). These CFs served as an eco-friendly substrate for the 
deposition of Cu2O/Ag2MoO4 nanocomposite catalysts (Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF), offering a sustainable and efficient 
method for the removal of reactive dyes, with specific focus on reactive blue 19 (RB19). The prepared hetero-
structure photocatalyst was thoroughly characterized using various analytical techniques, including XRD, UV–vis 
DR spectroscopy, SEM, TEM, TGA, photocurrent response, EIS measurements, PL spectroscopy, and FTIR spec-
troscopy. Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF photocatalysts exhibited exceptional photodegradation performance toward RB19, 
surpassing monomeric and binary materials 3.6–7.6 times. Enhanced performance resulted from improved 
visible-light absorbance and efficient e–/h+ pair separation. Mechanistic insights gained from radical scavenger 
experiments identify hydroxyl radicals (⋅OH) and holes (h+) as the primary active species responsible for pho-
tocatalysis. Reusability tests demonstrated the robustness and potential for multiple applications, maintaining a 
photocatalytic efficiency exceeding 86 % after five cycles, with negligible metal ion leaching. With superior 
visible-light photocatalytic performance, durability, and versatility, Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF can hold significant 
potential for addressing challenges in wastewater treatment and environmental remediation, contributing to a 
cleaner and healthier environment.   
1. Introduction 
In recent years, escalating global environmental concerns have 
precipitated an intensified quest for sustainable remedies to combat the 
pressing issue of environmental pollution. Among the myriad of envi-
ronmental contaminants, textile dyes have become a significant threat 
due to their persistence, toxicity, and harmful effects on ecosystems and 
human health [1]. Reactive dyes, a subgroup of vibrant organic 
compounds extensively used in textile coloring, form enduring covalent 
bonds with textile fibers, ensuring remarkable colorfastness. One such 
dye, Reactive Blue 19 (RB19; C22H16N2Na2O11S3), commonly known as 
Remazol Brilliant Blue R, is extensively used in coloring and printing 
cellulosic fibers [2]. Nevertheless, when wastewater containing RB19 
contaminates natural ecosystems or is used for irrigation, it poses risks to 
aquatic life, plant health, and human well-being. Contact with RB19 has 
been linked to various health conditions, including skin allergies,

exploring innovative and eco-friendly materials and technologies to

Photocatalytic methods have gained prominence in recent years as 
sustainable and efficient approaches for degrading organic pollutants, 
particularly textile dyes, in wastewater treatment. These methods are 
valued for their eco-friendliness, low energy consumption, high effi-
ciency, and minimal generation of harmful byproducts [4]. Among the 
diverse semiconductors explored for their potential as photocatalytic 
materials, Ag2MoO4 has emerged as a noteworthy candidate due to its 
outstanding electrical conductivity, antimicrobial properties, lumines-
cent characteristics, and photocatalytic activity. However, Ag2MoO4 
primarily responds to ultraviolet light due to its wide bandgap (>3.0 
eV), limiting its use in visible light photocatalysis [5]. To overcome this 
limitation, researchers have adopted strategies such as noble metal 
loading [6] and the fabrication of heterojunction structures [7,8] to 
broaden the light absorption range of semiconductors. Heterojunction 
structures, in particular, have proven highly effective, leading to sig-
nificant advancements in photocatalytic performance. Ag2MoO4′s 
unique characteristic of having notably low conduction and valence 
band positions compared to other semiconductors has enabled the 
construction of heterojunction photocatalysts by combining it with 
various semiconductors [9]. This synergistic approach effectively sepa-
rates photoinduced electron-hole pairs, extending their lifetime, and 
enhances the photocatalytic activity and stability of the resulting ma-
terials. Consequently, numerous heterojunction photocatalysts 
combining Ag2MoO4 with different semiconductors, such as ZnIn2S4/ 
Ag2MoO4 [10], Ag2CrO4/Ag2MoO4 [11], MgS/Ag2MoO4 [12], FeS2/ 
Ag2MoO4 [13], CoS/Ag2MoO4 [14], Ag2MoO4/Bi4Ti3O12 [9], Ag/ 
Ag3PO4/Ag2MoO4 [15], Fe3O4-C3N4-Ag2MoO4 [16], have been suc-
cessfully developed for the treatment of organic waste. 
Copper(I) oxide (Cu2O) is a p-type semiconductor with a narrow 
bandgap (1.8–2.5 eV) and a broad response range to solar spectrum 
[17]. Cu2O is considered a promising visible-light photocatalyst for 
constructing heterojunction photocatalysts aimed at achieving 
enhanced photocatalytic efficiency. It offers several advantages, 
including non-toxicity, environmental friendliness, low production 
costs, abundant resources, and high activity [18]. For example, Pahi 
et al. have integrated Cu2O with Ag3PO4 to create p-n heterojunctions for 
photocatalytic applications. The resulting Ag3PO4/Cu2O p-n hetero-
junction photocatalyst exhibited remarkable performance, with a 
hydrogen (H2) generation rate of 737.4 μmol g  1h  1 and an endosulfan 
degradation efficiency of 91 %, surpassing pure Ag3PO4 by 12 and 9 
times, respectively [19]. The p-n heterojunction photocatalyst demon-
strated improved electron-hole migration efficiency and higher current 
density, synergistically enhancing catalytic activity through the inter-
facial space charge junction. Similarly, Gao et al. designed S-scheme 
heterojunction photocatalysts based on Cu2O/BiOBr and studied their 
efficiency in photocatalytic degradation under saline solutions and real 
water conditions containing various organic dye contaminants [20]. 
This configuration enhanced the separation of photogenerated carriers 
and generated •OH radicals through the interaction of Cu(I) and H2O2, 
resulting in efficient photocatalytic degradation. 
In this context, the aim of this study is to enhance the photocatalytic 
efficiency for RB19 degradation by incorporating Cu2O/Ag2MoO4 
nanoheterojunctions. To further improve catalyst longevity and recy-
clability, a strategic immobilization onto cellulose fibers (CFs) derived 
from discarded cigarette butts (CBs) has been employed. This approach 
serves a dual purpose: preventing catalyst agglomeration to ensure 
sustained catalytic activity and enabling easy recovery, reducing mate-
rial loss. Cellulose-supported metal nanoparticles have found extensive 
applications in green and sustainable chemical transformations, allow-
ing for multiple recycling rounds without compromising their reactivity 
[21]. The choice of CBs waste as the source of CFs is based on the pre-
dominant presence of cellulose acetate, a primary constituent of CBs. 
This cellulose acetate can be readily converted into cellulose through 
alkaline treatment. Notably, approximately 6 trillion cigarettes are 
consumed worldwide annually, with approximately half of the CBs not 
disposed of properly [22]. These discarded butts, composed mainly of 
non-biodegradable plasticized cellulose acetate, toxic nicotine, and 
traces of heavy metals, present a significant environmental challenge. 
Repurposing them for environmental remediation aligns seamlessly 
with the principles of the circular economy and waste valorization, 
making a meaningful contribution to pollution control and resource 
conservation. 
2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Materials and chemicals 
Silver nitrate (AgNO3, 99.5 %), sodium molybdate dihydrate 
(Na2MoO4⋅2H2O, 99 %), ascorbic acid (C6H8O6, 99.7 %), reactive blue 
19 (C22H16N2Na2O11S3), potassium dichromate (K2Cr2O7, ≥ 99 %), 4-p- 
benzoquinone (BQ, 99 %), isopropyl alcohol ((CH3)2CHOH, > 98 %), 
and ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA, 99 %) were purchased from 
Merck. Copper sulfate pentahydrate (CuSO4⋅5H2O, 99 %), ethylene 
glycol (C2H6O2, 99 %), sodium hydroxide (NaOH, ≥ 98.0 %), hydro-
chloric acid (HCl, 37 %), and ethanol (CH3CH2OH, ≥ 99.8 %) were 
obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Company (Shanghai, 
China). 
2.2. Characterisation techniques 
The crystal structure was analyzed using X-ray diffraction (XRD) 
with a Rigaku Ultima IV diffractometer (Japan). Morphological char-
acteristics were observed under a field emission scanning electron mi-
croscope (FE-SEM) S-4800 (Hitachi, Japan) and a transmission electron 
microscope (TEM) JEM 2100 (JEOL, Japan). Spectral data, including 
Photoluminescence (PL), Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy 
(FTIR), and UV–visible diffuse reflectance spectroscopy (UV–vis DRS), 
were collected using various instruments: a Cary 4000 Eclipse Fluores-
cence Spectrometer (Agilent, USA), Bruker Tensor 27 spectrophotom-
eter (Germany), and Shimadzu UV-2401 PC UV–Visible 
spectrophotometer (Japan). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was 
performed on a LABSYS Evo TG-DTA instrument (USA) with a heating 
rate of 5 ◦C min  1, covering the temperature range from 50 to 900 ◦C. 
Photocurrent curves and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) 
Nyquist plots were acquired using a CHI 650E electrochemical work-
station in conjunction with a three-electrode cell immersed in a Na2SO4 
(0.1 M) electrolyte solution. The concentration of RB19 was determined 
using a Cary 60 UV–Vis spectrophotometer (Agilent, USA). The extent of 
contaminant mineralization was evaluated by analyzing the total 
organic carbon (TOC) data utilizing an Analytikjena Multi N/S 2100S 
TOC analyzer (Germany). Metal ion concentrations in the solution were 
determined using inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP- 
MS) with an Agilent 7700 instrument (USA). 
2.3. Synthesis of photocatalysts 
2.3.1. Preparation of CFs 
The CFs were prepared from CBs following a procedure outlined in 
our previous study [23]. In brief, the collected CBs were stripped of their 
outer paper wrapping, cut into approximately 1 mm-sized segments, and 
thoroughly washed with hot water. The CBs were then soaked in 50 % 
ethanol for 2 h to remove organic impurities and subsequently dried. 
Afterward, the dried CBs were immersed in a 5 % NaOH solution and 
heated to 60 ◦C for 4 h with continuous stirring. After treatment, the 
samples were filtered and rinsed with distilled water until reaching 
neutral pH. Finally, the resulting products were dried overnight at 60 ◦C.

2.3.2. Preparation of Ag2MoO4/CF 
Initially, 0.1 g of CFs were dispersed in 30 mL of distilled water using 
ultrasonication for 10 min. A solution (Solution A) was prepared by 
adding 0.5 g of AgNO3 to the CFs suspension and stirring for 1 h. 
Concurrently, another solution (Solution B) was created by dissolving 
0.36 g of Na2MoO4 in 30 mL of distilled water. The Ag2MoO4/CF 
nanocomposite was formed by slowly adding Solution B to Solution A 
while continuously stirring for 4 h. After synthesis, the product was 
separated via centrifugation, washed with distilled water and ethanol to 
remove impurities, and then dried at 60 ℃ overnight. 
2.3.3. Preparation of Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/Cellulose fibers 
To fabricate the Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF nanocomposite, the as- 
synthesized Ag2MoO4/CF product was utilized as the starting material. 
First, it was immersed in a 50 mL solution of 0.1 M CuSO4 and stirred at 
60 ◦C for 1 h. Subsequently, a slowly added 50 mL mixture of (0.5 M 
NaOH + 0.25 M ascorbic acid) solution was introduced, and the mixture 
was further stirred for 4 h. The resulting product was separated by 
centrifugation, followed by thorough washing with distilled water and 
ethanol to remove any residual impurities. The nanocomposite was then 
dried at 60 ◦C overnight. The process for fabricating Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF 
is visually depicted in Fig. 1. Additionally, to assess catalytic activity, 
comparative samples of Cu2O, Ag2MoO4, and Cu2O/Ag2MoO4 were also 
synthesized. Details of their synthesis processes are provided in the 
Supplementary Materials for reference. 
2.4. Catalytic experimental procedures 
The photocatalytic properties of the Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF nano-
composite was evaluated for the degradation of RB19 dye. A quartz 
reactor was employed for the photocatalytic experiments, where the 
prepared composite (100 mg) was dispersed into an RB19 solution (100 
mL, 25 mg/L). The reaction was performed at room temperature. Before 
irradiation, the suspension was stirred in the dark for 60 min to achieve 
adsorption–desorption equilibrium between the catalyst and the dye. 
Visible light, generated by a 250 W Xenon lamp equipped with a UV 
cutoff filter (λ > 420 nm), was then applied to the reaction mixture. 
Sampling at predefined time intervals (15 min) allowed for the moni-
toring of the degradation progress. The concentration of RB19 in the 
collected samples was determined using a Cary 60 UV–Vis spectropho-
tometer at 625 nm, enabling the assessment of degradation kinetics. 
Control experiments using Cu2O, Ag2MoO4, Cu2O/Ag2MoO4, and 
Ag2MoO4/CF followed the same procedure, providing comparative data 
for evaluating the photocatalytic activity of the Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF 
nanocomposite. Key factors examined encompassed solution pH, which 
ranged from 3 to 9, catalyst dosage varying from 0.5 to 3 g/L, and RB19 
concentration in the range of 15 to 45 mg/L. Data collected from these 
experiments were analyzed to ascertain the efficiency of the nano-
composite in degrading RB19 under visible light irradiation.

calculated energy band positions for Cu2O were   0.26 eV (CB) and +

Photoluminescence spectroscopy was employed to assess the 
electron-hole recombination behavior of the prepared materials. It is a 
well-established fact that the PL spectrum of semiconductor materials 
predominantly originates from the recombination of photo-induced 
charge carriers. Generally, a lower PL intensity signifies a reduction in 
the rate of charge carrier recombination [34]. As depicted in Fig. 6c, the 
PL spectrum of the Ag2MoO4 sample displayed three peaks at 485 nm, 
530 nm, and 542 nm, corresponding to specific electronic transitions 
within the MoO4 tetrahedra [35]. When combined with CFs, a notice-
able decrease in the PL intensity of Ag2MoO4 was observed. This 
reduction in PL intensity can be attributed to several factors. First, the 
presence of CFs may act as electron transport pathways, facilitating the 
migration of charge carriers and reducing their recombination rate, ul-
timately leading to a decrease in PL emission. Second, CFs may effec-
tively quench the excitons formed within Ag2MoO4, further contributing 
to the decrease in PL emission. Furthermore, with the addition of Cu2O 
to form the composite Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF, there was a further reduction 
in PL intensity compared to Ag2MoO4/CF. This observation demon-
strates that the level of electron-hole recombination is significantly 
constrained due to the heterojunction [36]. The decrease in the PL in-
tensity suggests that the inclusion of Cu2O enhances the charge carrier 
separation within the composite, resulting in fewer excitons reaching 
the radiative recombination pathways, and consequently, a decrease in 
PL emission.

3.2. Photocatalytic activity 
The photocatalytic activity of various fabricated photocatalysts was 
assessed in the visible-light degradation of RB19. Fig. 7a illustrates the 
comparative degradation of RB19 by Cu2O, Ag2MoO4, Ag2MoO4/CF, 
Cu2O/Ag2MoO4, and Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF. Evidently, photolysis of RB19 
exhibited negligible degradation, with only approximately 3 % of RB19 
removed over 150 min of visible light irradiation, indicating RB19′s 
relative stability under visible light. After 150 min of visible light 
exposure, the RB19 degradation efficiency was 49.1 %, 60.3 %, 66.7 %, 
74.3 %, and 98.9 % in the presence of Ag2MoO4, Ag2MoO4/CF, Cu2O, 
Cu2O/Ag2MoO4, and Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF, respectively. These results 
highlight that the incorporation of Cu2O significantly enhances the 
catalytic activity of Ag2MoO4, primarily due to the reduction in the 
recombination rate of electron-hole pairs. Notably, the ternary Cu2O/ 
Ag2MoO4/CF composite exhibited superior RB19 degradation compared 
to Cu2O, pristine Ag2MoO4, or Cu2O/Ag2MoO4. The attachment of the 
Cu2O/Ag2MoO4 nanocomposite onto CFs clearly proves to be effective 
due to the creation of an efficient solid–solid interface between Cu2O 
and Ag2MoO4 on the CFs’ surface. This interface is where the separation 
of photo-induced electrons and holes occurs most effectively, preventing 
their recombination and providing favorable conditions for complex 
photochemical reactions during RB19 degradation. By being immobi-
lized on the CFs, Cu2O and Ag2MoO4 nanoparticles can interact closely, 
allowing for efficient charge transfer and preventing the rapid

3.3. Effects of operating parameters and reusability 
Comprehending the influence of variables like catalyst dosage, RB19 
concentration, and pH levels on photocatalytic performance is crucial 
for enhancing the real-world applicability of Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF. In 
these experiments, C0 represents the RB19 concentration after 60 min of 
adsorption. The relationship between catalyst dosage and photocatalytic 
efficiency was investigated at various catalyst dosages (0.5, 1.0, 2.0, and 
3.0 g/L), as presented in Fig. 8a. It is observed that increasing the 
catalyst dosage from 0.5 to 2.0 g/L leads to an enhancement in photo-
catalytic efficiency from 57.6 % to 99.5 % within 150 min of illumina-
tion. This improvement can be attributed to the increased number of 
active sites provided by the catalyst’s surface as the dosage is increased 
[47]. However, when the catalyst dosage is further increased to 3.0 g/L, 
the RB19 degradation efficiency decreases to 73.5 %. This phenomenon 
may be attributed to an excess of catalyst, which could shield RB19 and 
light from reaching the catalytic centers [44]. Although the highest 
degradation efficiency is achieved at a 2.0 g/L catalyst dosage, the ef-
ficiency at 1.0 g/L is also comparable (97.1 %). Therefore, 1.0 g/L is 
considered the most effective catalyst dosage. 
The influence of pH on the RB19 degradation by Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF 
was investigated within the pH range of 3–9 under RB19 concentration 
of 25 mg/L and a catalyst dosage of 1 g/L. The experimental results, as 
depicted in Fig. 8b, unequivocally affirm that the initial pH of the RB19 
solution significantly impacts the catalytic activity of the material. The 
RB19 degradation efficiency after 150 min of reaction is found to be 
58.8 %, 76.9 %, 97.1 %, and 47.8 % at pH values of 3, 5, 7, and 9, 
respectively. This trend suggests a distinct pH-dependent behavior. The 
observed behavior can be attributed to the point of zero charge (PZC) of 
Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF, which is determined to be 7.6 (as presented in 
Fig. S1 of supplementary information). Below the pHPZC (pH 3), the 
catalyst’s surface becomes positively charged, enhancing the adsorption 
of negatively charged RB19 molecules. While this stronger adsorption 
theoretically favors photocatalytic activity, it can reach a point where 
excessive adsorption creates a dense RB19 layer on the catalyst’s sur-
face. This layer, in turn, limits the penetration of light into the catalyst, 
reducing the overall efficiency of the photocatalytic process [40]. 
Conversely, at pH values above the pHPZC, the catalyst’s surface becomes 
negatively charged, leading to repulsion between the catalyst and RB19 
molecules, resulting in reduced adsorption capacity. However, the 
relatively higher pH values (pH 7) bring the surface charge closer to 
neutrality, optimizing the catalyst’s performance by maintaining a bal-
ance between adsorption and photocatalytic activity. 
The influence of the initial concentration of RB19 on Cu2O/ 
Ag2MoO4/CF photocatalysis is crucial due to the varying pollutant 
concentrations in industrial wastewater. Experiments were conducted at 
different initial RB19 concentrations (15, 25, 35, and 45 mg/L) while 
maintaining a constant pH of 7 and a catalyst dosage of 1 g/L. Fig. 8c 
reveals a significant trend: as the initial RB19 concentration increases, 
the photocatalytic efficiency of Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF decreases. This 
decrease in efficiency can be attributed to several factors. At higher 
RB19 concentrations, competitive adsorption onto the catalyst’s active 
sites intensifies, reducing interactions between RB19 molecules and 
active sites and impeding the photocatalytic process [49]. Mass transfer 
limitations may also occur at elevated RB19 concentrations due to the 
high concentration of RB19 molecules in the bulk solution, decreasing 
the contact time between RB19 molecules and the catalyst’s surface 
[50]. Additionally, beyond a certain RB19 concentration threshold, the 
catalyst’s active sites may become saturated with RB19 molecules, 
leading to diminishing returns in terms of photocatalytic efficiency. 
Furthermore, at higher RB19 concentrations, the dye molecules can 
absorb a significant portion of incident light, limiting the amount of light 
available for photocatalytic reactions [44]. This light absorption phe-
nomenon can result in reduced photocatalytic efficiency as the RB19 
concentration increases. 
The long-term stability and reusability of the Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF

stability, a series of RB-19 degradation cycles was performed, as illus-

successive degradation cycles, the photocatalytic activity of Cu2O/ 
Ag2MoO4/CF remains remarkably consistent, with the RB-19 degrada-
tion rate consistently exceeding 86.48 %.

4. Conclusions 
The successful synthesis of Cu2O/Ag2MoO4 nanoheterojunctions 
supported on CFs has been rigorously confirmed through a compre-
hensive series of characterizations. This novel composite has exhibited 
remarkable photocatalytic prowess in the degradation of RB19 under 
visible light. Notably, the ternary Cu2O/Ag2MoO4/CF composite has 
outperformed its constituent parts, Cu2O, pristine Ag2MoO4, or Cu2O/ 
Ag2MoO4, by a substantial margin, achieving degradation rates 3.6–7.6 
times higher. This remarkable enhancement can be primarily ascribed to 
the composite’s enhanced visible-light absorbance and its ability to 
effectively separate and transfer photo-generated electron/hole (e–/h+) 
pairs. Following 150 minof visible light irradiation, the 1.0 g/L Cu2O/ 
Ag2MoO4/CF photocatalyst successfully degraded 98.9 % of the 25 mg/ 
L RB19 dye solution at pH 7. Furthermore, the research has underscored 
the substantial influence of pH and catalyst dosage on the catalytic ac-
tivity of the ternary composite. The outcomes of free radical capture 
experiments have unequivocally highlighted the pivotal roles played by 
hydroxyl radicals (⋅OH) and holes (h+) in the photocatalytic process, 
whereas superoxide radicals (⋅O2–) exhibited negligible effects. Further-
more, reusability experiments have convincingly demonstrated the 
composite’s robustness and its potential for repeated utilization. Even 
after undergoing five consecutive cycles, the composite maintained a 
photocatalytic efficiency exceeding 86 %. In summary, the Cu2O/ 
Ag2MoO4/CF ternary composite emerges as a highly promising photo-
catalyst for the degradation of RB19. It boasts superior visible-light 
photocatalytic performance, durability, and the potential for wide-
spread applications in wastewater treatment and environmental 
remediation.
