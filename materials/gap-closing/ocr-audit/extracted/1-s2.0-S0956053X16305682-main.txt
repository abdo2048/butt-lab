3. Results and discussion
3.1. Cellulose pulp production
Cigarette butts contains four different components: (i) a ﬁlter
made from cellulose acetate with a myriad of chemicals adsorbed
in it, (ii) unburned tobacco, (iii) ash and (iv) paper. In order to
develop an environmentally friendly ﬁnal disposal method for
cigarette butts, a process was proposed to produce cellulose pulp,
which can be used in the recycled paper market. Indeed, the main
idea was to hydrolyze cellulose acetate into cellulose in the pres-
ence of strong alkali, depicted in Fig. 3 . The use of a strong base
may also remove lignin from unburned tobacco in the cigarette
butt and thus liberate the cellulose from this residue, as well as
dissolve the ash and disaggregate the remaining paper. Thus, at
the end of the process, cellulose was obtained as a solid phase as
well as a solution containing all the non-cellulose components of
the cigarette butt.

butts at 80 /C176C under different conditions: using distilled water for
6 h; NaOH solution (0.5%) for 10 min, 20 min and 150 min; and
NaOH solution (1%) for 10 min. When cigarette butts were cooked
using distilled water, no signiﬁcant change in the FT-IR spectra was
