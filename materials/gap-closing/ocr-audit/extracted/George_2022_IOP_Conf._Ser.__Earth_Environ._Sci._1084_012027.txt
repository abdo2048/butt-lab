Review on Recycling of Microplastics in Cigarette Butts 
 
Mary George1 and Rupali Khadtar2 
Anjuman-I-Islam’s Abdul Razzak Kalsekar Polytechnic 
Email-mary.george@aiarkp.ac.in1,rupali.khadtar@aiarkp.ac.in 
 
Abstract. It's a lesser-known fact that the cigarette filter is made of cellulose acetate, a 
kind of non-biodegradable plastic and it takes almost 10 years for this ingredient to  
completely decompose. Cigarette smoking is on the rise throughout the world. Ever  
since tobacco smoking be gan there are both opponents and proponents for the same.  
Instead of getting into a discussion as to whether one should smoke or not; would  like 
to focus on cigarette butts which a micro plastic that is harmful to the environment and 
needs to be disposed of in an appropriate manner to conserve the environment and human 
health. As the trend is today, cigarette smoking is on the increase. And in light of  this 
event recycling cigarette butts give rise to a sustainable material. 
This paper will cover how ciga rette butts can be collected and recycled to tackle the  
unfriendly and unsightly remains of cigarettes after smoking. 
Keywords: Cigarette butts,  Solutions to handle cigarette butt  waste, Awareness 
regarding probable health and environmental hazards 
 
1. Introduction 
Filters were initially used from 1860 to 1920 to prevent particles of tobacco from entering the mouth. In the 
1950s after published studies began to reveal the health threats of tobacco, the cigarette industry propagated 
the use of cigarettes with filters. The use of filters gave cigarette smokers  an illusion that they are safe from 
the ill effects of cigarette smoking and started inhaling as much or more tar, nicotine, and noxious gases as 
smokers of unfiltered cigarettes. The fast life of ci ties and the increased pressure in day-to-day life due to 
the industrial revolution causing men to compete with machines has caused many men to smoke to relieve 
stress and tension. Consequently, there is an increased litter of cigarette butts. 
 
2. In what way are cigarette butts harmful? 
2.1. What are micro plastics? 
Micro plastics comprise tiny fibers from nylon clothes and other synthetic textiles. Plastic particles smaller 
than 5 millimeters are considered to be micro plastics. Well, it is likely that micro plastics continue breaking 
down even past 10nm. Plastics do not blend with the soil easily but  remain in the soil in a particulate 
manner, probably just becoming into even smaller pieces. The tinier the particle, the easier it is for them 
to pass cell membranes and cause tissue damage. For example, it is observed that micro plastics can absorb 
toxic chemicals and then release them into an animal’s digestive system. This would definitely be harmful 
to our health. [1] 
Research has shown these nano plastics can enter the intestines during digestion. Whether they can 
intermingle with the blood is not clear, however, another rather unnerving study demonstrated that      nano 
plastic particles lodged in the brains of fish affected their behavior. The study found that

2.2 What are micro plastics in cigarettes made up of? 
The majority of cigarette filters are made of cellulose acetate, which is a type of plastic. The cigarette filter 
is not cotton, but a plastic that does not disintegrate similar to other types of plastic  in the environment. 
Cigarette butts (CB) are one of the most common types of litter in cities, with  an estimated 4.5 trillion 
thrown each year, accounting for22-46 percent of visible litter. 76 percent of cigarettes smoked in public in 
metropolitan areas are littered rather than disposed of properly. Butts are then carried to coastal locations 
by rain or river water, where they are typically the most  abundant part of litter collected. The chemical 
makeup of cigarette butts waste has an impact on the environment. Because cigarette butts are made up of 
a highly tw isted network of fibers  combined with plasticizers, the breakdown is sluggish and difficult. 
Furthermore, toxic metals and  nicotine have been found to seep out of cigarette butts. The Lethal 
Concentration 50 for test fish exposed to cigarette butts' leachates is one cigarette butt immersed in a liter 
of water for 96 hours. When this trash is released into the environment, it poses a significant risk to living 
species and ecosystem health. As a result, cigarette butts pose a threat to the environment and public health. 
[2] 
 
3. Where are cigarette butts found? 
Cigarette butts are tossed on the street or littered in parks, and beaches, they may be thrown in street gutters 
too. Since indoor smoking is banned by many organizations smokers have been pushed outdoors. Cigarette 
butts are mostly found near corporate offices, hospitals, colleges , and tourist destinations. Also, outside 
bars cigarette butts are found in large quantities.

5. What is harmful about cigarette butts? 
Cigarette butts take anywhere from 18 months to ten years to decompose. 
Chemicals and heavy metals, the same things that kill smokers, leak into the environment when butts are 
discarded. They frequently wash into the gutters, polluting our rivers and endangering fish and other 
creatures. 
The most serious issue is the filters, which are mostly comprised of cellulose acetate, a type of plastic. That 
means the filters take a long time to biodegrade, even when they do, thousands of tiny plastic fibers wind 
up in the environment, streams, and wildlife. Arsenic, benzene, and lead are among the substances found 
in the fibers. 
Cigarette butts are hazardous plastic fragments that are frequently mishandled and contain over 15,000 
separate strands of plastic fiber. Cigarette butts can be carried into rivers and lakes,  eventually ending up 
in the ocean. This source might potentially release 300,000 tons of micro-plastic fibers into the aquatic 
ecosystem each year. 
Furthermore, harmful compounds such as nicotine, carcinogenic tar, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons 
have a highly toxic effect on aquatic creatures, causing serious damage. 
According to the WHO, tobacco product waste contains over 7,000 toxic chemicals. Cellulose acetate, the 
major component of cigarette butts, along with paper and rayon, pollutes land and water. A report 
by Truth Initiative, a U.S.-based NGO says that the chemicals that leach from a single cigarette butt can 
release enough toxins to kill 50% of saltwater and freshwater fish exposed to it for 96 hours. [3,4] 
There is also the fear of birds, fish , and animals eating cigarette butts that can be disastrous,”  viii. As a 
result, it is vital to comprehend the mechanism by which cigarette butts release micro 
Plastic fibers, the potential environmental impact, and viable mitigation techniques for cigarette butt litter. 
[5] 
 
6. How is the cigarette butt processed for recycling? 
The cigarettes get shredded and separated into 2 key parts, the organic matter which is the ash, tobacco, 
and paper composted and the inorganic component which is the filter is melted. 
The paper is ground in an industrial grinder and treated with an organic binder. The ground  material is 
spread into sheets and dried. They are later cut and sold in local shops as mosquito  repellant. The paper 
with remains of nicotine act as a mosquito repellant. To use the mosquito repellant, it just has to be burnt. 
The plastic in the cigarette butt is also ground in the industrial grinder, they are then soaked  in sterilizing 
chemicals for 24 hours which leaves them looking like cotton. Later the plastic fibers are fluffed and made 
to the required specifications. 
It is not necessary that the plastic from cigarette butts be recycled separately. It can be combined with other 
plastics such as polyethylene and polypropylene. Cellulose acetate is found in objects 
like combs, toothbrushes, playing cards, tool handles, pen barrels, etc. made from plastic. Hence all these 
plastics can be recycled together. [6] 
 
7. What can be made from cigarette butts? 
 Composted organic waste from cigarette butts is used as fertilizers. 
 At present in India cigarette butts are recycled and used to make mosquito repellants and soft toys. 
 Butts is also used to make packing and building material as well as plastics for key chains and 
toys and cushion. [7] 
 In France the fibers from cigarette butts are used to make jackets, here the plastics obtained from

8. How cigarette butts can be used in the construction industry. 
Cigarette butts with leftover tobacco had an energy value of 16.5 MJ/kg. If just 2.5% of all bricks produced 
annually worldwide included 1% CB content, all of the CBs currently produced could be recycled in bricks, 
and it is estimated that global firing energy consumption could be reduced by approximately 20 billion MJ 
(mega joules). This is roughly equal to the annual energy consumption of one million houses in Victoria, 
Australia. “Firing butts into bricks is a reliable and practical way to deal with this terrible environmental 
problem, while at the same time cutting brickmaking production costs,” Mohajerani said. 
Cigarette butt bricks are also lighter and provide superior insulation, resulting in lower heating and cooling 
expenditures in the home. Mohajerani’s research team discovered that once cigarette butts were employed 
on the pavement, they could not only withstand high traffic, but it could also minimize heat created by the 
sun hammering down on black asphalt, a phenomenon known as the urban heat-island effect. It was 
discovered that butts encapsulated in bitumen and combined with class 170 bitumen performed better in 
heavy traffic than paraffin-encapsulated butts mixed with c170 and c320 bitumen after completing various 
tests on the samples. The butts encased in paraffin were barely able to withstand light traffic. [9] 
Insulating material that can be used in the construction industry can be made from the  plastic in cigarette 
butts.

9. Is recycling cigarette butts a small business? 
About 6 trillion cigarettes are produced every year, leading to more than 1.2 m illion tons of cigarette butt 
waste. These figures are expected to increase by more than 50 percent by 2025, mainly due to an increase in 
the world population. 
It is estimated that 4.5 trillion cigarette butts are disposed of annually. An estimated 766,571 metric tons of 
cigarette butts make their way into the environment every year. 
 
10. Has the litter of cigarette butts reduced wherever an attempt has been made? 
By the financial year 2019-2020 a whopping 10,000 kg of cigarette waste, of which 40 percent has been 
recycled and utilized. Each kg of cigarette waste comprises 4,000 cigarette butts. A company by the name 
CODE has pioneered in India recycling cigarette butts. Having started in 2016 in Noida so far  the duo, 
Vishal Kanet and Noman Gupta have  successfully collected 500 -600 kilograms of cigarette  waste and 
recycled 40-50 kgs. CODE stands for Conservation of the depleting environment. 
 
11. Who should give a go-ahead to the project? 
Non-smokers should give a go -ahead for this method of recycling micro plastics. When smokers are  
careless about the harm they do to their own bodies  by smoking, non-smokers who strongly oppose this  
habit should propagate the ill effects  of the micro plastic  environment and come up with innovative  
methods of freeing the environment of this nuisance. 
12. Scope of this innovative method 
Before anything can be done technically to recycle cigarette butts, education has to play a major role in this 
field. Non-smokers who understand the harm of cigarettes should take the lead in recycling cigarette butts. 
Social stigma should not be feared and a bold and confident approach should be  taken to recycling this 
notorious element. Smokers should be educated about the harm of cigarette butts and should be encouraged 
to dispose of cigarette butts in the proper manner. The misconception that cigarette butts are made of cotton 
which is biodegradable should be changed and people need to know that  cigarette butts are ma of non-
biodegradable micro plastic. Fines may be imposed on those who are careless in this matter. Government 
should finance start-ups in this area and motivate them towards a clean India and world. 
13. Conclusion 
Like every drop makes an ocean, every cigarette butt adds up to the litter which amounts to 4.5 trillion 
individual butts polluting our global environment. Due to a long history of the tobacco industry targeting, 
low-income communities of color, they have greater smoking rates and confront systemic disparities  that 
make it difficult for them to access health  information and resources. Furthermore, they are  
disproportionately served by inequitable waste management,  which can exacerbate the consequences  of 
cigarette butt pollution. Hence recycling cigarette butts makes a  sustainable innovative solution  to the 
problem.
