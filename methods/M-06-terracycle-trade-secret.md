## 0. Summary card
TerraCycle (Trenton, NJ, founded by Tom Szaky) ran the first worldwide cigarette-butt collection and recycling program from 2012, first sponsored by Santa Fe Natural Tobacco, later rolled out to Australia (2014), Canada and France (Zero Waste Boxes). The disclosed outline: collected butts are shredded; tobacco and paper are composted; the cellulose-acetate filters are dried, powdered/refined into pellets, and compounded by contracted third-party processors into industrial plastics — shipping pallets, plastic lumber, benches, ashtrays. The company explicitly restricted outputs to industrial products, not household plastics, because of nicotine exposure. Priority on the registry because it is the only route documented at city/multi-country logistics scale. But: the actual sanitize/decontamination chemistry is trade-secret and mineable from no patent; every process number (temperatures, tonnages, % recycled content) is `parameter_not_located` in the public record; and the operator's credibility is dented by a settled false-advertising suit (Jan Dell, 2021) and the Bloomberg 2022 investigation into its contracted-processor model — which the team has now READ IN FULL (v0.2): TerraCycle does no recycling itself (all reprocessing contracted out, no independent recycling audit until 2021), revenue $71M (2021, company-stated), contaminated flakes downcycled into playground surfaces/outdoor furniture, and tracker evidence of collected material ending in landfill. [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5][S6] **v0.3 (session 6, wave-2 sources): the sanitize step is CLOSED** — TerraCycle's collection partner Surfrider (SF chapter, 2017) discloses gamma-radiation sterilization as the first process step, followed by shredding, tobacco/paper composting under tobacco-composting laws, and filter blending/pelletization; ~150,000 butts make one industrial pallet. First quantity data on record (brigade capture 2015-04-23: 6,172 locations, 25,768,769 units collected). The program CONTINUES in 2026 as the all-brands, Santa Fe-sponsored Cigarette Waste Free Recycling Program. The full process chain is now sourced end-to-end (launch → collection → sterilize → shred/split → pelletize → end-use restriction). [S7]-[S14] **v0.4 (session 7, four-agent arena research + live fetches): Wish 1 CLOSED with a divisor caveat** — KAB 261,000 lb (2024) = 118.4 t as reported, but the official 1 lb = 1,000 butts divisor is proven an accounting convention three independent ways (composition, field weighing, TerraCycle's own yield line all give 0.20 g/butt), so real cumulative mass is ~52 t; the brigade FAQ page is FOUND (logistics-only — no mass, no gamma; its totals footer yields a 14-point growth curve, 45,089,591 units by 2015-12-20). **Wish 2: dose/dwell is a FIRM NEGATIVE** — zero patents (USPTO), zero filing mentions: the parameters are withheld by design, not unfound; bioburden testing corroborated (company fact sheet + Belmont 2021 pending browser verify); five public gamma-position variants documented, model = whole-stream-first per the R&D VP. Capture rate ~0.016% computed — collection is the binding constraint. [S15]-[S26]

## 1b. Session-6 wave-2 evidence (v0.3) — the process chain is now sourced end-to-end
[S7] = `csp-2012-santa-fe-launch` (CSP Daily News, 15.11.2012); [S8] = `njcom-2012-trenton-butts` (Times of Trenton, Simpson interview, 13.12.2012); [S9] = `plasticsnews-2012-butts` (Shawn Wright, 16.11.2012, full text via Wayback 2014 capture — the operator's live-page PDF is paywalled to the lede); [S10] = `surfrider-2017-litter-faq` (partner FAQ, fetched live 25.11.2026); [S11] = `terracycle-brigade-2015` (Wayback capture 23.04.2015); [S12] = `terracycle-cigwaste-free-program` (live page 25.11.2026); [S13] = `terracycle-recycling-process` (live page); [S14] = `terracycle-community-faq` (live page).

1. **Launch anchors** — Santa Fe Natural Tobacco Co. (Reynolds American subsidiary) was the EXCLUSIVE sponsor from day one [S7]; program name Cigarette Waste Brigade; Szaky's framing: a solution for filters that are "properly disposed of in an ashtray or can, but today still end up in a landfill" [S7].
2. **Process chain, named-source version** (Ernie Simpson, TerraCycle VP R&D) [S8]: deconstruct the cigarettes → compost tobacco and paper → process the filters → blend cellulose acetate with other recyclables → pellets → plastic lumber, pallets, bins, ashtrays. ~97% of the filter is cellulose acetate ("a lot of playing cards are made from cellulose acetate"). Development took "about six months" per Simpson [S8] vs "nearly two years" per Plastics News [S9] — discrepancy on record, unresolved. First test material came from TerraCycle's own offices and neighbors (Albe Zakes) [S8].
3. **THE SANITIZE STEP — CLOSED** [S10]: Surfrider SF (a TerraCycle collection partner) FAQ: "The collected cigarette waste is first sterilized using Gamma radiation, which removes toxins and bio-contaminants." Then the material is shredded; remaining tobacco and paper are composted "with strict adherence to tobacco composting laws"; shredded filters are blended with other recycled materials and pelletized. "Roughly 150,000 cigarette butts go into the formulation of one pallet." LCAs by PE International and Five Winds Strategic Consulting show 20-80% carbon savings vs landfill/incineration scenarios. TerraCycle's OWN pages still never mention gamma — the trade-secret framing narrows to: mechanism disclosed by a partner, parameters (dose, dwell, validation records) remain undisclosed.
4. **Quantities** [S11]: brigade page capture 23.04.2015 — 6,172 collection locations, 328 open slots, 25,768,769 units collected; SFNTC donates $1.00 per pound to Keep America Beautiful's Cigarette Litter Prevention Program. Accepted waste: extinguished cigarettes, filters, loose tobacco pouches, outer plastic + inner foil packaging, rolling paper, ash; paperboard box packaging excluded. Units are check-in counts, not mass; tonnage remains `parameter_not_located`.
5. **Current program (2026)** [S12]: Cigarette Waste Free Recycling Program — free, all brands, Santa Fe-sponsored; FedEx QR code or UPS label; box ≤ 50 lb and ≤ 18x18x18 in; odor guidance (remove residual tobacco, keep dry). TerraCycle's own process line: "Once collected, the cigarette waste is cleaned and separated by material type. The materials are recycled into raw formats that manufacturers use to make new products. The ash and tobacco are separated out and composted in a specialized process." No sterilization mention. [S13] adds the company-wide frame: MRF check-in (scan date/weight/material), manual non-compliant sort-out, chain-of-custody tracking, sortation technologies (manual, size, sink/float, optical/NIR, air density, magnetic), plastics melted into pellets/flakes/powder; company claims >95% regional processing and 98.3% of received materials recycled; end products include pallets, outdoor furniture/decking, flooring, playground surfaces. [S14] repeats the cleaned-and-separated line plus the third-party-verified-LCA / recycling-guarantee claim.
6. **Incentive mechanics** [S9]: 100 TerraCycle points per pound shipped, redeemable at 1 cent per point for the charity of choice — the collection-side economics datapoint.

1. **Mass row closed with a caveat** [S15][S16][S17]: "more than 261,000 pounds ... since 2014" (KAB, 2024) = 118.4 t reported floor; official divisor "one pound of cigarette waste being equal to 1,000 cigarette butts" (SFNTC, 2018, ">100 million pieces"); TerraCycle's own Carnival line (42.5M butts → "over $42,500") self-confirms the 1,000/lb book rule. BUT three independent routes — filter composition (0.194 g cellulose acetate/butt), Marquette's weighed shipment (14,583 butts = 6.43 lb = 2,268/lb), and Simpson's "about a thousand cigarette butts to produce 100 grams of plastic" (~52% mass yield) — converge on **0.20 g/butt**, so the book divisor inflates true mass ~2.3x: **real cumulative mass ~52 t, not 118 t**. Participant nodes agree with 0.20 g, not with 0.454 g: DYNA-MIG ~600k butts ≈ 500 lb (~1,200/lb) [S26]; C-TRAN ~2,000-4,000 butts per 1-2 lb. Carry BOTH numbers, labeled; never publish 118 t bare.

Invariant across all five: **gamma somewhere in the chain + bioburden/bacteria testing**. Witness ranking: the R&D VP outranks comms quotes → model M-06 row 1 as whole-stream sterilization upstream of mechanical separation. 2012 processing location: Portland, Pa. [S19] — corroborates the Ultra Poly contractor hypothesis (name unconfirmed).

## 4. Safety notes
- **The route's own output is nicotine-bearing**: the operator restricted end uses because of nicotine exposure — treat all pellets/lumber from this route as chemically contaminated, not clean. [S2]
- **Compost stream is hazardous**: tobacco/paper compost carries nicotine, TSNAs, metals — this is not clean garden compost; lawful disposal/treatment boundary required (registry F1 sink rule applies). [S1][S2]
- **Stored used butts** are a pathogen/odor burden; any handling needs hygiene controls; the sanitize step being unknown means no assumed safety. [S1]
- **No DIY replication**: nothing here is a recipe; the only safe team activity is logistics study and product assay of existing outputs.
- **Stop-condition**: metals uncharacterised (row 10).
- Desk research authorizes no physical trial; qualified facility, risk assessment, lawful waste handling, named human actor.
