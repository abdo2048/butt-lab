## 0. Summary card
Slow pyrolysis of whole cigarette butts in a 200 g fixed-bed reactor at 650/700/750 degC (25 degC/min heating, N2 60 ml/min, 5-10 min hold after target temperature, three repetitions) splits the butt into pyrolysis oil (37.92-39.52 wt%) carrying the plasticizer triacetin (GC-MS area 31.23/33.26/42.99% at 650/700/750 — max at 750 degC, usable as an oxygenated biodiesel additive only after purification), a calcium-rich porous char (25.68-27.45 wt%; Ca rises from 2.15 to 31.85 wt% with temperature; pores < 1 um; CaCO3 identified by FTIR), and gas (33.03-36.40 wt%, CO2-dominated: 11.6% CO2, 8% CO, 4.6% H2, 2% CH4-class). Priority because it is the only route that needs NO decontamination chemistry — whole butts go in as collected (feedstock supplied by Keenat Company, France — cross-link M-04); the toxic load is destroyed or concentrated into the char/oil streams, which are themselves the products or process fuel. v0.2 upgrade: the FULL paper is now on the shelf (`sources/yousef-2023-jaap/`, operator-supplied PDF + conversion) — the earlier abstract-only flags are resolved; remaining unknowns are narrowed to char metals, quantified oil toxicology, gas calorific measurement, and economics (declared "under research" by the group). A companion line of literature carbonizes butts into porous carbons (230-900 degC variants; KOH-activated carbon reaching 2726 m2/g surface area). [S1][S2] **v0.3 cross-check against the post-dating Chemosphere 2026 critical review (Zhang et al., now on shelf): it CONFIRMS the Yousef numbers (yields, triacetin 43% at 750 degC) and explicitly frames the 42.99% as ENRICHMENT WITHIN the mixed oil, not isolated purity; field-level verdicts match our open rows exactly — no complete nicotine/PAH/metals mass balance in ANY of 37 screened studies, no pilot scale anywhere (200 g fixed bed = largest whole-butt reactor), no complete TEA, and conventional pyrolysis judged the near-term baseline route. It adds the staged-decomposition frame: triacetin desorbs at 100-300 degC (separable stage), so 650-750 degC is reactor set-temperature, not release temperature.** [S1][S2][S4]

## 1. What the sources disclose — step by step
[S1] = Yousef, Eimontas, Zakarauskas, Striugas, "Pyrolysis of cigarette butts as a sustainable strategy to recover triacetin for low-cost and efficient biodiesel production", J. Anal. Appl. Pyrolysis 175 (2023) 106167, DOI 10.1016/j.jaap.2023.106167 — **FULL TEXT on shelf** (`sources/yousef-2023-jaap/document.pdf` + `text.md`; operator-supplied 2026-11-25; was abstract-only before). Funded by Research Council of Lithuania S-MIP-23-118; authors at Kaunas University of Technology + Lithuanian Energy Institute; data availability declared confidential. [S2] = shelf reviews: `torkashvand-2019-review` and `shah-2023-review` (carbon-material lines). [S3] = registry entry `pyrolysis-energy-recovery`.

1. **Feedstock** — whole cigarette butts supplied by **Keenat Company, France** (the M-04 operator; paper's acknowledgment + ref [64] keenat.com) with some cigarette packaging (plastic film, cardboard) removed by hand; dried naturally for a couple of days, stored ventilated; used AS COLLECTED — no crushing/milling, explicitly to avoid dust and upscaling costs. Batch composition measured: **62 wt% filter, 20 wt% paper, 18 wt% tobacco**; tobacco carries 2.6 wt% N (paper + filter ~N-free), which the authors use to argue low NOx on combustion. [S1]
2. **Reactor and conditions** — integrated fixed-bed pyrolysis system, 200 g electric furnace, capsule with grid (char retained, oil/gas pass); four scrubbers + cooling traps (isopropanol, saturated salt water) for tar/aerosol capture; online gas analyser (Zambelli ZB1 IGD) plus GC-TCD (Agilent 7890A) and GC/MS (7890A/5957C); SEM-EDX (BPI-T, 20 kV) and FTIR (32 scans, 8-10 cm-1) for char. Temperatures 650/700/750 degC, **heating rate 25 degC/min**, **N2 60 ml/min** from the top, **hold 5-10 min** after target temperature until the IGD reading stabilizes; each run **repeated three times**. Conditions inherited from the group's prior TGA/kinetic study of CBs (Yousef et al. 2022, Biomass Conv. Bioref., DOI 10.1007/s13399-022-02698-5), which found components fully degrade 650-700 degC; 750 degC added to guarantee full decomposition of variable-composition feed. [S1]
3. **Product yields (Table 1, exact triplets)** — 650 degC: oil 39.52 / char 27.45 / gas 33.03; 700 degC: oil 38.33 / char 26.61 / gas 35.05; 750 degC: oil 37.92 / char 25.68 / gas 36.40 wt%. Each row sums to 100.0 (99.99 for 700 degC) — **the v0.1 mass-balance concern is RESOLVED**: the abstract's loose ranges were rounded cross-temperature values, not an inconsistent balance. Temperature shifts yield oil→gas mildly (4-10% relative). [S1]
4. **Oil composition (Table 2, GC-MS area %)** — triacetin is the dominant single compound at every temperature: **31.23% (650) / 33.26% (700) / 42.99% (750)** (the abstract's "43%" = the 750 degC value). The rest of the oil is characterized: nicotine-derived alkaloid peaks are PRESENT — pyridine, 2-(1-methyl-2-pyrrolidinyl)- 11.17% (650) and its (S)-3-isomer 10.47% (700) / 7.95% (750); N-Methyl-2-furohydroxamic acid 7.21% (700); phenol 2.2-3.4%, 3-methylphenol up to 5.47%; 2-hydroxy-3-methyl-cyclopentenone up to 8.63%; furans, maltol, monoacetin (1,2,3-propanetriol monoacetate 4.08-5.75%) and diacetin 4.12% — i.e., triacetin partially deacetylates into mono/diacetin in the oil. The v0.1 inference (row 2) is CONFIRMED DIRECTIONALLY: the oil is NOT a clean fuel — it carries alkaloids and phenols and needs purification before any blending claim. [S1]
5. **Gas composition** — online IGD across the whole run: CO2 11.6 wt% and CO 8 wt% (formed <550 degC, T-insensitive), CH4 2% and H2 4.6% (H2 rises with temperature, dips slightly at 750 due to side reactions). GC sampling (Tedlar bags, six samples per batch at 19-43 min) corrects the IGD: the "CH4" channel folds in C2H6/C3H8; light hydrocarbons appear at moderate temperatures; H2 dominates late at 750 degC. Paper's disposition: burn the gas for plant power (refs 65-66) or separate via membranes (refs 49-51, the group's own line). [S1]
6. **Char (Table 3 EDX, area scan)** — carbon 82.50 (650) → 56.25 (700) → 26.87 wt% (750); **calcium 2.15 → 13.87 → 31.85 wt%** (main inorganic, from tobacco smoke constituents and tobacco-substitute sheet, refs 57-59); potassium 1.72 → 6.02 → 7.15; at 750 degC also Fe 4.48, S 3.04, Cl 1.35, Si 1.09, Mg 1.21, P 0.76 (Cl 1.88 and Mg 1.28 already at 700). FTIR: CaCO3 bands (876, 711 cm-1), glycosidic 980, aromatic C=C 1396, CO 1968-2152, and a surface-OH band 3641 cm-1 at 700/750. Morphology: debris at 650, flakes at 700/750; pores < 1 um. Authors' proposed use: adsorbent (Ca-rich char literature, refs 61-62). v0.1's inference that Ca concentrates from paper filler/ash is CONFIRMED as CaCO3 by FTIR; the paper adds K and minor elements. [S1]
7. **Circular-economy framing (paper's own)** — collection exists at industrial level (companies like Keenat, ref 64); plant can be fed whole butts (no pre-treatment); gas powers the plant; char as adsorbent; oil blended up to 25% because the allowed triacetin rate in biodiesel is 10% (cited to ASTM D6451 [sic — likely ASTM D6751; flag kept] and EN 14214). The authors declare an LCA "highly required" (future work) and a techno-economic analysis "under research" by the group. [S1]
8. **Carbon-material companion line** [S2]: charred carbon via 900 degC/3 h pyrolysis, N2 100 cm3/min, 5 degC/min heating rate — surface area 597 m2/g (micro 453 / meso 144 m2/g, pore ~3.32 nm, HNO3-modified for Pb adsorption: 74.5 mg/g with KOH vs 66 mg/g without; methylene blue 88.77 mg/g); single-stage 900 degC/1 h — 637 m2/g; vacuum 230 degC/3 h then KOH — 117.2 m2/g; KOH activation at 900 degC — 2726 m2/g BET, mesopores 2.48-2.65 nm; N-doped supercapacitor carbon 330.1 F/g at 0.5 A/g, 93.48% retention after 10,000 cycles; hydrogen-storage carbon up to 9.4 wt% H2 (best-in-class claim, Blankenship & Mokaya 2017). 16% of cigarette mass lost during activated-carbon production (Yang 2017).
9. **Emissions context** [S2, Torkashvand citing Mohajerani 2016]: heating CB-bearing material increases CO, CO2, HCN emissions vs no-CB material — relevant to any thermal route's flue-gas treatment. Note the JAAP study measured NOx-relevant nitrogen input (tobacco 2.6 wt% N) but did NOT report HCN or NOx measurements — the HCN concern stays unmeasured.

`parameter_not_located` across [S1]: quantified nicotine/PAH mass in the oil (GC-MS area % is semi-quantitative, not wt% of oil); char Cd/Pb/As/Hg (EDX area-scan does not resolve trace metals); gas calorific value (composition only, no CV); purification cost for the additive claim; continuous-run data. Declared by authors: LCA (future) and techno-economics (under research).

## 1b. Chemosphere 2026 critical review — post-dating cross-check (v0.3)
[S4] = Zhang H., Fu J., Wang B., Hu H., Zhang L., "Thermal conversion of waste cigarette butts: Feedstock heterogeneity, process control, valorization pathways, and environmental safety: A critical review", Chemosphere (2026) 145131, DOI 10.1016/j.chemosphere.2026.145131 — **FULL TEXT on shelf** (`sources/chemosphere-2026-145131/`: operator-supplied 18-page PDF + GLM conversion + 11 figures). 66 references; 37 studies screened against four environmental-safety endpoints.

1. **Confirms our primary numbers** [S4 ↔ S1]: whole-butt pyrolysis 650-750 degC → oil 38-39.5 wt%, char 25.7-27.7, gas 33-36.4; triacetin highest at 750 degC reaching 43%; gas at 750 degC CO2 11.6 / CO 8.0 / H2 4.6 / CH4 2.0 — all match the JAAP full text on our shelf. The review adds the interpretation the abstract lacked: the 43% is ENRICHMENT WITHIN the mixed oil, not isolated purity or per-run yield — the caveat our rows 2/8 already carry is now externally anchored.
2. **Staged-decomposition frame (new, process-relevant)** [S4]: triacetin and other additives thermally desorb at 100-300 degC; cellulose acetate main pyrolysis runs 301-400 degC with acetic acid as main product (H. Kim 2024). Catalytic desorption stage (Al-MCM-41) + NON-catalytic CA pyrolysis → ~40% overall acetic acid yield; catalysis during the CA stage cracks the acetic acid and promotes coke. 650-750 degC (Yousef) is the reactor set-temperature for whole-butt conversion, not the triacetin release temperature. Team implication: a TWO-STAGE scheme (low-T additive desorption, high-T conversion) is the literature-consistent way to recover additives; the JAAP one-stage design trades selectivity for simplicity.
3. **Adjacent routes with numbers** [S4]: filters-only pyrolysis 500 degC fixed bed → 53.3% oil (Lee 2025); smoked butts 400 degC → 33.3% liquid fuel, +dolomite → 28.6% liquid, gas +8 wt%, HHV 12.8 → 18.1 MJ/kg (Duanguppama 2023); oxygen-fed updraft gasification + 900 degC steam catalytic reforming (KATALCO 57-4GQ) → syngas yield 82.3-89.7%, H2 30.27 vol% after reforming, tar 18.15 → 13.38 g/m3 (Yousef 2025 — the same group's follow-up); Ni/char pyrolysis-reforming 700 degC → 75% organics conversion, coke lowest at 700 degC (Guo 2025); slow pyrolysis 400-700 degC → char 26.6 → 20.1 wt%, 500-600 degC chars BET 272.9-270.8 m2/g with Ni(II)/Cu(II) adsorption (Premchand 2025); 350 degC inert + KOH activation → BET 433 m2/g, Cr(VI) 55.8 mg/g (Herrera-Puerta 2025).
4. **Field-level verdicts (anchor our open rows)** [S4]: within 37 screened studies — NO complete mass balance of nicotine/PAHs/native metals across gas/oil/char/ash/wastewater; NO standardized leaching tests on chars; NO ecotoxicity or pyrolysis-oil toxicity assessments; NO pilot-scale demonstration (the ~200 g fixed bed is the largest whole-butt reactor; gasification-reforming reports no continuous capacity); NO complete TEA or comparable unit costs; NO independent LCA with butts as the sole feedstock (Nongdren 2026 excluded thermochemical routes; Mahmud 2026 used mixed waste, so footprint is not attributable to butts). The review's own routing conclusion — conventional pyrolysis as the near-term baseline route with highest scale-up potential — matches ours.
5. **New open rows added** [S4]: (a) cellulose acetate microfiber release is quantified BEFORE conversion (5.78-92.43 items/g over 1-60 d exposure, Mohammadi 2025) but never compared before/after thermal conversion — thermal decomposition must not be read as microplastic-risk elimination; (b) activation/pretreatment wastewater (KOH, H3PO4, ZnCl2, ethanol prewash) is never characterized for nicotine/PAH/metals loads — an uncosted waste stream in every functional-carbon route; (c) feedstock heterogeneity (smoking status, brand, weathering, collection source, pretreatment) shifts decomposition windows by ~40-50 degC and leachate COD by up to 314% — any team replication must define and publish its feedstock boundary (ours: Keenat-style whole butts, 62/20/18 wt%).

## 4. Safety notes
- **750 degC furnace + toxic feed**: off-gas contains CO (measured 8% of product gas), HCN risk (tobacco nitrogen; unmeasured in this study), PAH-laden tars in the condenser line; flue treatment and CO/HCN monitoring mandatory. [S1][S2]
- **Pyrolysis oil is a hazardous chemical product**: nicotine-derived alkaloids 7.95-11.17% area + phenols confirmed in the oil — flammable, toxic, handle under fume extraction; NOT a fuel additive until assayed and purified. [S1]
- **Char dust**: Ca-rich (up to 31.85 wt% Ca as CaCO3) but metal-bearing (Fe 4.48, trace metals uncharacterised — row 4 stop-condition); respirable dust controls; characterize before any use claim. [S1]
- **KOH activation line** (companion [S2]): molten/alkaline KOH at 900 degC is severely corrosive — qualified lab only.
- **No DIY pressure/thermal equipment**; the 200 g reactor is a lab furnace construct, not replicable outside a qualified facility.
- Desk research authorizes no physical trial; qualified facility, risk assessment, lawful waste handling, named human actor.
