## Explicit Q1–Q5 answers

**Q1 — Product class.** F0/F1/F3/F4 aim at recovered acetate or fibre; F2 dilutes milled filters into a thermoplastic composite; F5 converts acetate to cellulose/paper. A coherent “cleaning” claim exists only for extraction/partitioning families in the limited sense that they create a separate contaminant-bearing stream: F0 water/ethanol, F1 water/sludge, F3 solvent/water/residue, F4 dense-fluid concentrate, F5 dark liquor/precipitate. None has a source-supported clean-product claim, because no independent analyte-specific product assay, leachate test or waste-stream mass closure was located[2,3](@ref). F2 encapsulation is not a cleaning claim: the toxic fraction either remains in the composite, leaves in unmeasured off-gas/condensate or goes to an uncharacterised reject[7](@ref). “Not disclosed” dominates: no family is shown by a located product assay to fail, but no family is shown to meet a product criterion. The Korean paving-stone case is a secondary comparator for encapsulation, not a whole-family failure proof[20](@ref). F5’s material conversion is chemically coherent, but conversion is not detoxification; the dark liquor still needs a lawful sink[10](@ref).

**Q2 — Toxic fraction.** F0: hot/cold water and ethanol washes, plus casting-solvent and drying residues; no disclosed treatment sink. F1: first/decontamination wash water, 8% sludge, paper/tobacco/ash rejects, dust/off-gas and product end-of-life; MéGo! recycles water with no purge, TerraCycle sends tobacco/paper to compost and filters to lumber, Code Effort sends tobacco/ash to compost, Re-Cig names an external purification partner, CNR names concentrated pollutant water, Cerdia’s effluent is not in the abstract[5,6](@ref). F2: F2 rejects, heating/extruder off-gas, condensate, CSS combustion emissions and composite leachate; no disclosed sink. F3: ethanol/acetic-acid extracts, aqueous washes, hot-separation residue, polymer-containing filtrate and vapours; full accounting missing. F4: 0.3% concentrate, 100 mL effluent per 30 L fibre, spent sorbents/filters and separated tobacco/paper; closed loop claimed but no disposal route or assay. F5: dark liquor, acid precipitate, wash water and residual solids; HCl precipitation/recycle reported but precipitate disposal and effluent treatment unresolved[10](@ref). Families with no disclosed sink are F0, F2 and F4; F1/F3/F5 name partial sinks but none quantifies them.

**Q3 — Temperature/pressure plausibility.** For Keenat, keep three boundaries separate: the patent’s 80–100 °C step b, the inherited press quote of “successive heatings ~200 °C”, and a later extrusion stage whose profile is undisclosed[7,15](@ref). At 80–100 °C, drying plus partial volatilisation is plausible; thermal destruction of nicotine, PAHs and pesticides depends on residence time, oxygen and catalysis, not merely a boiling point. Metal behaviour is species-specific, so neither “all metals volatilise” nor “metals are non-volatile” is defensible[7,16](@ref). For TchaoMegot, the patent gives nested envelopes, not one setpoint: 0–2000 bar, preferably 50–350, preferably 280–320 bar; CO2’s stated critical reference is at least about 31 °C and 73.85 bar[9](@ref). A press quote of ~50/60 bar at ~30 °C is below both critical values, so it describes dense/subcritical CO2, not supercritical CO2, unless the quote is wrong or refers to another stream[11,15](@ref). That is a provenance/phase mismatch, not proof of physical impossibility. F0’s 60 °C drying, F3’s 75–95 °C/2–4 h granted acid-stage condition and F5’s unquantified hot alkaline pulping likewise must not be merged into whole-process conditions[4,8,10](@ref).

**Q4 — One discriminating measurement for the whole batch.** Use **one standardized, dry-mass-normalised contaminant mass-balance plus product-leachate challenge** on the same homogenised smoked-butt feed, applied as six comparator arms by qualified laboratories, with no operating instructions inferred here. For each analyte *i* (nicotine, representative TSNAs and PAHs, Cd/Pb/As/Hg, residual process solvents and product-contact leachate), measure mass in feed `M_i,feed`, product `M_i,product`, each liquid concentrate, each solid residue and captured gas/condensate. Compute closure `C_i,j = ΣM_i,outputs / M_i,feed` and product retention `R_i,j = M_i,product / M_i,feed` for family *j*, with blanks and spike recovery as analytical quality checks. This discriminates by **partition vector**, not a single pass/fail number: F0/F1 should load water/ethanol or sludge; F2 product/off-gas/reject; F3 solvent/water/residue; F4 dense-fluid concentrate/sorbent; F5 dark liquor/precipitate. A family that claims cleaning but shows high `R_i,j`, poor `C_i,j`, or leachate without a quantified purge/disposal has no clean-product claim. A family with low product retention, high recovery in a named lawful sink and acceptable product leachate would separate itself from encapsulation and from routes that merely move contaminants into sludge or vapour.

**Q5 — What would change my mind.** I treat **F5 as strongest in evidence structure**, not as proven clean, because it names a toxic concentrate and a chemical conversion rather than relying on appearance or encapsulation. I would upgrade it only after obtaining the full primary Teixeira paper, a dry-mass pulp yield, dark-liquor COD/salt/metal/nicotine/TSNA data, acid-precipitate hazard classification and disposal records, and an independent pulp/leachate assay with mass closure; I would downgrade it if recycle accumulates contaminants or the paper retains them[10](@ref). I treat **F2 as weakest**, because the alleged toxic fraction has no disclosed sink, off-gas and condensate are unmeasured, product leachate is unassessed, and the operator’s own reported LCA retreat plus the Korean comparator caution against the encapsulation logic[7,19,20](@ref). I would upgrade F2 only with a controlled off-gas/reject/product mass balance, composite leachate and microplastic data, and an independent LCA showing benefit under a declared boundary. Conversely, product retention of carcinogens or nicotine-bearing condensate would confirm the weakness without needing to claim a universal family failure.

## What the packet gets wrong or overstates

1. **It is not a worldwide or quality ranking.** Batch selection chose six families for mechanism diversity and falsifiability, with English/French/Portuguese-heavy access and inherited Chinese/Korean/Spanish leads; no register search was performed[13](@ref). Correction: treat F0–F5 as a purpose-selected comparison, not “the six best”.
2. **The evidence is substantially same-author.** The manifest says the packet contains hypotheses, attributed company claims and prior same-author readings, not verdicts; reused specs and `gap-check-01.md` are prior readings, and v1.1 is a bounded QC correction, not independent replication[2,3,17](@ref).
3. **“Verified-source” labels can be misread.** The packet’s own reading limits say no independent contaminant assay, leachate test, emission measurement or LCA report was located; company pages are self-report; full patent specifications and regulatory texts were not supplied[2,3](@ref). Correction: retain “source says it” rather than “process proven”.
4. **Residual stronger language remains.** The manifest flags “directly undermines” in batch selection and “direct precedent” in the Keenat spec as overstatements; the errata withdraws “most concentrated liquid effluent”, “depolymerisation”, “most transparent/honest” and the blanket “metals are non-volatile” claim[2,16](@ref). Correction: use the errata as authoritative for those changes.
5. **Product and process denominators are repeatedly conflated.** “92% recovery”, “99.7% clean material”, “80% CA split”, “100% utilisation” and “~39% polymer” are recovery, company split, ratio or utilisation claims, not detoxification, purity or dry-mass yield[5,6,8,9,10](@ref).
6. **“No solvent wash” wording is hazardous to interpretation.** The manifest explicitly warns that CO2 avoiding a conventional aqueous/organic wash must not be read as the literal absence of a dense fluid; the company map likewise says “sans eau” is not “dry”[2,11](@ref).
7. **Legal and certification language is not analytical evidence.** Patent legal status is a Google Patents database assumption with no register query; ISO, End-of-Waste, REACh, INERIS and “medical-grade” signals do not state which analytes were measured at what limit in the product[6,11,12](@ref).
8. **The cautionary dossiers are secondary.** The INERIS, Keenat-LCA and Korean paving-stone notes are project summaries, not primary reports; I independently checked INERIS HTML/HAL pages, but did not obtain the Keenat LCA report, Korean study or any PDF[18,19,20,27](@ref).
9. **Primary-source access was thinner than the citation list implies.** Several Google Patents fetches exposed only front pages/abstracts; the De Fenzo Methods and supplementary numerics were not exposed in the fetched pages; full WO/FR descriptions, Cerdia full text, CNR PDF, Re-Cig patent number and TerraCycle partner identity were not obtained[12,13,22,23,24,26](@ref). Correction: mark those as access gaps, not negative results.
10. **F1/F3 company variants are not one mechanism.** The dossier itself identifies three product logics — recovered/re-melted CA, CA destruction to cellulose/paper, and filler encapsulation — with different exposure pathways; they cannot be ranked on one cleanliness number[6](@ref). I have retained the packet’s F0–F5 taxonomy and introduced no replacement taxonomy.
