## 2.2 Per-proposal deep dive

### P-01 — F1/F0 hybrid: segregated first-displacement concentrate with a written composition envelope

**Mechanism.** F1's disclosed sequence grinds, sieves, washes in a wash tank, re-washes in a decontamination tank, recycles the water, dries and mills (`specs/mego-fr3066128a1-source-reconstruction.md` §2–3; the recycle step is quoted in the FR3066128A1 abstract). F0's disclosed sequence adds hot-water washing at 50 °C for 60 minutes, three cold-water washes and two 99 % w/w ethanol washes (`specs/de-fenzo-2020-source-reconstruction.md`, §"Disclosed process"). In both, the wash water is the carrier into which the water-soluble load — above all nicotine — moves. In F1 that carrier is closed: "recycling the water (26) of steps c) and d) by a water recycler" with **no purge disclosed** and no treatment technology named. A closed loop with no purge cannot be a sink: it can only redistribute and concentrate. The proposed change is to make the loop *leaky in a designed, measured place*. One wash fraction — the first displacement, before any recycle — is bled off to a segregated, weighed, sampled collection vessel. That fraction is then treated as a **product with a specification**: its composition (nicotine, TSNAs, PAHs, Cd/Pb/As/Hg, COD) is measured on a stated basis and written into a composition envelope, and the *route for that stream is then chosen by the data* rather than by default. Nothing in the packet supports the bleed point, the envelope, or the routing logic — those are mine.

**Source basis versus assumptions.**

| Element | Label |
|---|---|
| F1 grind→sieve→wash→decontaminate→recycle→dry→mill sequence | source-supported (FR3066128A1 abstract, via packet spec §3) |
| F0 hot-water 50 °C/60 min, 3 cold washes, 2× ethanol 99 % w/w washes, 60 °C/60 min drying | source-supported (De Fenzo §2, via packet spec) |
| "Recycling the water" with no purge, no treatment train, no accumulation control named | source-supported (FR3066128A1 abstract; packet spec §3 step 6, §6) |
| MéGo! 1 kg ≈ 2,000 (or 4,000) butts; 12 % paper/tobacco/ash, 8 % sludge, 80 % CA, denominator unstated | source-supported company self-report (fresh-mass basis, denominator unresolved — Stage-3 conflict C-04) |
| Water-soluble organics partition preferentially to the first displacement (least-loaded-solid, highest-gradient step) | inference |
| Nicotine is water-soluble and is the driver of the HP6 classification of the feed | source-supported (my fetch: INERIS 2018 committee report excerpt; Rebischung et al. 2018, *Waste Manag.* 82:9–14) |
| First-displacement liquor is the fraction carrying the bulk of the water-soluble load | inference |
| Bleeding one fraction before the loop bounds the concentrate volume | inference |
| A concentrate can be given a composition envelope and routed by its measured composition | new-assumption (mine) |
| The bleed point, envelope and routing logic | new-assumption (mine) |
| The toxic mass is concentrated in the tobacco/ash fraction rather than the filter fraction | **not assumed** — this is P-01's central open question and the falsifier |

**Change proposed and the Stage-3 blocker it addresses.** The Stage-3 blockers are: (1) no dry-basis mass balance; (2) no analyte-resolved closure; (3) named streams presented as final sinks without outlet-performance evidence; (4) identity/purity/detox/leaching/usability conflated; (9) no comparable energy/reagent/cost evidence; (10) the batch-wide discriminator. P-01 addresses **(2) and (3) directly**: it creates one stream whose mass and composition are measured by design, so the four-field sink accounting for the toxic fraction can be answered with data rather than with a named stream. It addresses **(1) and (9) partially**: a segregated, weighed bleed vessel converts an unmeasured loop into a measured term in a dry-mass balance, and it makes the waste route a costed line item rather than a rounding error. It does **not** address (4): P-01 produces a CA with a *measured residual burden*, and whether that burden is acceptable is a separate, use-specific gate (§2.4), deliberately not folded into the sink claim.

**Failure modes with severity.**

1. *The concentrate is a large dilute stream rather than a small concentrated one* — **fatal**. If the first displacement carries the nicotine at a concentration too low to be classified and too high in total mass to be landfilled, the design has produced a large-volume effluent problem with a small convenient label. INERIS's own position makes this sharp: because butts are HP6/HP14 dangerous waste, a nicotine-bearing aqueous stream inherits hazardous-waste handling, and INERIS records that storage in waste-storage installations is excluded because the total organic carbon (COT) of the butt waste exceeds acceptability thresholds (search-excerpt of the INERIS 2018 committee report; the underlying PDF was not opened by me).
2. *The toxic mass is in the tobacco/ash fraction, not the filter fraction* — **fatal to P-01 as specified**, but not to the architecture. The tobacco/paper/ash fraction carries the smoke-derived load (nicotine, TSNAs, PAHs) far more plausibly than the CA bulk, and F1 already separates it. If that is where the mass is, the correct design is simpler and better (see §2.3 Q4): send the ash fraction to the sink, keep the wash loop closed with a small measured purge. P-01's bleed point would then be aimed at the wrong stream.
3. *Recycle re-partitions contaminants into the CA* — **fixable**. A later, dilute wash fraction that is recycled contacts progressively cleaner fibre; nicotine and other water-solubles that re-adsorb during that contact raise product content over cycles. Measurable by a cycle-series loop assay.
4. *The bleed fraction carries recoverable CA with it* — **fixable**. The first displacement mobilises fines, and fines are CA. The "sink" may then be exporting product value. Recoverable by solids recovery in the bleed train, at a cost.
5. *The sink route is unlawful or unaffordable in practice* — **unknown**. No operator in the batch has produced a disposal record, an End-of-Waste dossier or a permit file for a butt-derived concentrate; and the INERIS classification is French, so the route is jurisdiction-specific (Stage-3 gap G-19).

**Falsifiers.** (i) A **partition ranking** on one homogenised feed: the tobacco/ash fraction versus the first wash liquor, each expressed as a share of total feed analyte mass (dry basis, same feed, same panel). This is a ranking, not a pass/fail, and it decides P-01's bleed point without any threshold. (ii) A **closure-with-uncertainty** test on the loop: if the loop's measured analyte mass over successive cycles rises without a defined purge, the closed-loop claim in the disclosure is falsified as a sink. (iii) A **volume-versus-value** comparison: if the bleed fraction's mass flow of toxicants is so large that no lawful route is affordable below the recovered-CA value, P-01 is economically void regardless of its chemical correctness.

**Needed calculations/measurements.** (a) The dry-basis, analyte-resolved fate balance of §2.3/Q3, with the wash train sampled as *first fraction / later fractions / recycle / purge*, so that the bleed term is explicit. (b) A cycle-series loop assay (≥3 cycles) measuring nicotine and TSNA in loop water, in the CA product, and in the bleed fraction, on a stated basis. (c) The product gate of §2.4 applied to the recovered CA for a **declared, restricted use** — non-food, non-skin-contact — including an ecotoxicity endpoint, because a chemical panel alone cannot clear HP14. (d) A weighed dry-mass balance including the bleed vessel's contents, the solids, the drying loss and the mill fines.

**Equipment/waste implications.** Equipment classes added relative to disclosed F1: one segregated collect vessel with a weigh bridge (the minimum instrumentation that makes a sink measurable), a solids/liquids separation step for the bleed fraction, sampling and laboratory submission, and an interface to a licensed waste contractor. No solvent, no pressure vessel, no caustic — this is the cheapest route in the batch to iterate a sink design, which is a deliberate part of the choice of F1 as the starting point. Waste: the bleed fraction is a nicotine-bearing aqueous stream and, by the INERIS position, a dangerous-waste stream (HP6 by calculation from nicotine; HP14 only by ecotoxicological test) — traceability, approved transport, and one of: a hazardous-waste thermal treatment facility, or a material route **only if** the fraction separately satisfies NF U 44-051 *and* an added TSNA/ecotoxicity panel (see §2.4, where I show the NF U 44-051 tabulated criteria do not cover TSNAs). Unused rejects (paper/tobacco/ash) are the other fraction and must be routed separately — the soil route INERIS itself identifies for the compostable fraction requires analysis against NF U 44-051, and the plastic fraction requires an end-of-waste determination via installations classées. I give no operating instructions for hazardous-waste processing and no dosage, temperature or procedure.

### P-02 — F3 hybrid: metal-first partition into an acid phase

**Mechanism.** F3 dissolves the acetate, filters the insoluble matrix, and re-precipitates the polymer; CN106928486B's disclosed pretreatment rationale is explicit and unusually useful — ethanol to remove solanesol-type impurities, deionised water to remove nicotine, washing to a 2 µS/cm conductivity endpoint (CN106928486A/B via packet spec §"Source-reported parameters"; granted-claim distinction in `reports/gap-check-01.md`). CNR's offering describes acetone dissolution, solid filtration, water antisolvent precipitation, acetone recovery by boiling, and "concentrated pollutant water" to treatment (`specs/company-workflow-dossiers-batch01.md` §6). The logic that makes F3 the batch's clearest *separation* concept is that insolubles are rejected at filtration by construction, and solvent-borne organics are expected to stay in the mother liquor. The slot-B question F3 has never answered is **metals**. Cd and Pb do not volatilise in a thermal route and do not partition into plain wash water; they are carried by the inorganic/ash fraction. F5's dark liquor is reported to carry metals; F4's own patent design space reportedly includes chelating extractants for heavy metals (both as Stage-3 slot-B retrieval leads, not packet facts). P-02's change is to make the metal partition an **explicit, measured design function of the acid stage** — so that the metals ride out in a small liquid concentrate and the precipitated polymer is metal-depleted — and to accept the consequence if that fails.

**Source basis versus assumptions.**

|---|---|
| CN106928486A/B: 8–18 mol/L acetic acid; room temperature to 95 °C; 0.25–4 h (description) vs 75–95 °C / 2–4 h (B claim 1); 1–6 L acid per kg of cigarette ends; 58–92 % recovery assertion; wash endpoint 2 µS/cm | source-supported (packet spec; granted-claim distinction from `gap-check-01.md`, a prior same-author note) |
| The ethanol/water impurity rationale (solanesol; nicotine) | source-supported as a disclosed rationale via the Stage-3 slot-B machine-translated fetch (retrieval lead, official text not confirmed) |
| CNR: acetone dissolve → filter → water antisolvent → boil off acetone → concentrated pollutant water to treatment | source-supported as an offering-page statement (self-report) |
| Insolubles are rejected at filtration by construction | inference (chemical, not a measurement) |
| Metals reside predominantly with the inorganic/ash fraction | inference |
| Metals will partition to the acid phase in the F3 chemistry | **inference, explicitly unmeasured** — the load-bearing assumption of P-02 |
| A deliberately metal-driving acid step (rather than incidental partition) | new-assumption (mine) |
| The metal-bearing acid phase can be characterised as a small, classifiable concentrate | new-assumption (mine) |
| The 58–92 % figure is polymer recovery, not purity or detoxification | source-supported as a distinction (packet spec §"What the source supports and does not support") |

**Change proposed and the Stage-3 blocker it addresses.** P-02 targets blocker (2) and the metals half of blocker (3): the batch-wide discriminator demands Cd/Pb/As/Hg closure across product, liquids, solids, off-gas, purge and unexplained loss, and **no family in the packet has a metals gate at all**. Stage-3's slot-B predecessor already flagged co-precipitation as an unmeasured hypothesis (B-29) and the resolution-denominator as unresolved (G-06). P-02's contribution is to say that the metals, not the nicotine, are the class that decides whether a recovered-CA product class is viable at all — and to make that a designed, measured function with a stated falsifier.

**Failure modes with severity.**

1. *Metals do not partition to the acid phase* — **fatal for a CA-recovery claim**. If Cd/Pb stay with the polymer or with the filtered residue, then no washing, dissolution or compaction route can produce a metal-compliant CA, and the honest conclusion is that the CA-recovery product class requires a non-CA product class. This is a possible outcome, not a defect in the design.
2. *Co-precipitation carries nicotine/TSNA onto the polymer* — **fixable**. Solvent-borne organics can co-precipitate or be entrained on the precipitating polymer; this is inference (Stage-3 B-29), and it is directly measurable as product-vs-mother-liquor analyte mass.
3. *Solvent and acid recovery never close; the aqueous waste dominates* — **fatal**. CNR's own sequence creates a large dilute acetone–water stream, and the 1–6 L/kg acid loading on a wet/dry basis that is itself unresolved (`gap-check-01.md`) means the reagent inventory is unquantified. If the acid/acetone loop does not close, the "clean" fraction is paid for with a solvent-bearing aqueous waste stream, and the sink design is worse than F1's.
4. *Residual solvent or acid in a contact product* — **fixable**, and material for the product gate.
5. *The 2 µS/cm washing endpoint is read as clearance* — **fixable but must be stated**: conductivity is an ionic endpoint and says nothing about neutral organics (TSNAs, most PAHs) or metal speciation. It is a process endpoint, not an assay.
6. *F3's own operating conditions remain unresolved (blocker 5)* — **unknown**, and unresolvable at desk: the granted B text was never read and the A PDF fetch failed.

**Falsifiers.** (i) An acid-phase vs precipitate vs filtered-residue metal mass balance on one feed (Cd, Pb, As, Hg by ICP-MS, dry basis). If the metals do not follow the acid phase, P-02's premise fails and the product class must change. (ii) A product-vs-mother-liquor organic balance (nicotine, TSNAs, representative PAHs) to settle co-precipitation. (iii) A solvent- and acid-recovery mass balance per tonne of feed to test whether the reagent loop closes. (iv) A residual-solvent assay on the precipitated polymer.

**Needed calculations/measurements.** (a) Full-stream analyte and metal balance: precipitate / acid mother liquor / washes / filtered insolubles / recovered solvent / vapour. (b) The dry-basis recovery denominator, which the packet records as unresolved (G-06) — recovery must be reported as dry CA mass out ÷ dry acetate mass in, **never** as "92 % clean". (c) Residual solvent and residual acid in the product. (d) Product gate for a declared use (§2.4), including the ecotoxicity endpoint.

**Equipment/waste implications.** Equipment classes: dissolution vessels with corrosion duty, filtration, an antisolvent/precipitation stage, a solvent-recovery unit, and the analytical suite for metals and organics. Permitting reality: flammable-fluid (acetone) and acid regimes, exhaust ventilation for solvent vapour, and a pressure-vessel regime only if the recovery step operates under pressure — I treat all of this as an engineering/permitting question and give no operating instructions. Waste: the metal-bearing acid phase is the toxic sink; it is likely the most expensive stream in the batch to route, because trace metals are the main driver of hazardous-waste acceptance cost alongside the nicotine/TSNA load. The filtered insoluble residue (paper/ash) needs its own route and must not be assumed to be a compost feedstock.

### P-03 — F5, modified: purge-sized closed liquor loop with a separately characterised precipitate

**Mechanism.** F5 hydrolyses the acetate groups to cellulose and produces a dark liquor carrying lignin, carbonyls, metals, nicotine and tobacco-specific nitrosamines, which is acid-precipitated with HCl and partly returned to pulping (`specs/unb-pi0305004-source-reconstruction.md` §2, §5). F5 is the only family whose toxic stream is quantified anywhere in the packet, and it is the only one where the *reason* the sink fails is documented: the Stage-3 slot-B retrieval lead gives COD 29,986 mg/L in the dark liquor and treatment removals of approximately 20 % (acid precipitation), 66 % (chitosan coagulation) and 45.8 % (ozonation), and states that the clarified effluent was proposed for reuse in alkaline pulping **because the residual COD was still high**. That is not a sink; it is a recirculation with a rising concentration, unless a bleed exists. P-03's change is to size the bleed from the balance: measure the contaminant mass entering the liquor per tonne of dry feed, measure the removal achieved by the chosen treatment on the actual liquor, compute the residual that the loop must discharge to remain at steady state, and make that discharge an explicit, permitted, assayed stream. The acid precipitate — the true concentrate, and the stream whose composition the packet records as "not characterised" — is separately assayed and separately routed rather than treated as an incidental byproduct. The pulp gets its own gate, because a paper product may contact skin and is the highest-surface-area product in the batch.

| Element | Label |
|---|---|
| F5 mechanism: NaOH deacetylation to cellulose, lignin/ash/chemicals dissolved, pulp washed, dark liquor, HCl precipitation, liquor returned to pulping | source-supported (author summary; via packet spec §2–3) |
| Dark liquor contains lignin, carbonyls, metals, nicotine, tobacco-specific nitrosamines | source-supported as a secondary description (De Fenzo 2020 citing Teixeira), via packet spec §8 |

**Change proposed and the Stage-3 blocker it addresses.** Blocker (3) (streams named as sinks without outlet performance) is P-03's direct target: the recycle is currently doing the work of a disposal route, and the only quantified treatment performance in the whole packet belongs to this family, so this is where the packet is closest to being answerable. Blocker (2) is addressed by requiring the liquor balance to carry analytes, not only COD: COD is a lumped parameter and does not tell you whether nicotine, TSNAs or metals are being removed. Blocker (5) (F5 primary full text not obtained) is *not* addressed by P-03 and remains the family's dominant documentary gap.

**Failure modes with severity.**

1. *The pulp retains nicotine/TSNA/metals for a skin-contact paper use* — **fatal** for that use. No pulp or paper assay was located in any source read (Stage-3 B-26).
2. *The purge needed exceeds the liquor flow* — **fixable**. If the required steady-state bleed is comparable to the liquor generation rate, the "closed loop" is not a loop and the process is a batch effluent process with a large water footprint; that is a design finding, not a failure of chemistry.
3. *COD outlet not achievable at a lawful standard and cost* — **unknown**. The primary treatment study was never obtained; the packet supports a serious unresolved burden, not a demonstrated failure (Stage-3 judge resolution of C-07).
4. *The precipitate is the dominant toxic mass and is unclassified* — **fixable** once assayed; until then it is a probable hazardous-waste stream with no route.
5. *Alkali/acid handling and gas release* — an industrial-hygiene and permitting question the packet already flags (spec §6); I add no procedure.

**Falsifiers.** (i) A closed-loop liquor balance over successive cycles with measured purge, COD, nicotine, TSNA and metals in and out: if concentration still rises, the loop is a concentrator and P-03 fails as stated. (ii) A pulp leachate and pulp-content assay against a declared skin-contact criterion. (iii) A precipitate assay plus a jurisdiction-specific hazard classification.

**Needed calculations/measurements.** (a) Dry-feed-to-pulp/liquor/precipitate/wash balance, per outlet, with COD, TOC, nicotine, TSNAs and metals; (b) the purge-rate calculation from (a); (c) pulp content and leachate; (d) product gate including ecotoxicity.

**Equipment/waste implications.** Caustic and acid handling, gas-risk assessment for acidification, effluent permitting for any bleed, and hazardous-waste transport for the precipitate. This is the highest-permitting-cost route in the batch, which is why it is P-03 and not the primary proposal: a sink design that must be demonstrated at pilot scale before it can be operated is a poor place to start iterating.

## 2.3 Answers to Q1–Q5

### Q1 — Starting point

**F1 (mechanical separation + aqueous washing) as the starting point for the sink architecture, combined with F5's liquor-treatment logic for the concentrate; P-02's F3 chemistry as the route that tries to fix the metal class.** On evidence-structure grounds, not optimism:

- *The missing sink is named in F1's own disclosure.* FR3066128A1's abstract specifies recycling the wash water and specifies no purge and no treatment train. That is the rare case where the Stage-3 blocker is located in the source text rather than inferred from silence, which makes it the cheapest thing in the batch to iterate against. Every other family's sink gap is an argument about what is *not* said.
- *F1 has the widest industrial base and the most data.* Four operators with three different product logics (recovered-and-re-melted CA, CA-destroyed-to-paper, filler encapsulation — `specs/company-workflow-dossiers-batch01.md` §8.1), plus the only published mass split (MéGo! 80/12/8, fresh-mass basis, denominator unresolved) and the only published polymer yield (Re-Cig ≈38.7 % and 39.1 % of collected mass, company accounting, no moisture or reject term). Two independent operators' numbers already show that the denominators are incompatible, which is the symptom the Stage-3 judge asked to be fixed.
- *F1 carries the lowest permitting and capital burden.* No solvent, no pressure vessel, no caustic. For a stage whose job is to design and characterise a sink, the cheapest-to-iterate train is the right starting point, and I say so as an engineering sequencing judgement, not as a claim about the family's merit.
- *The toxic mass is most plausibly concentrated in a separable fraction.* Smoke-derived nicotine, TSNAs and PAHs originate in the tobacco/paper fraction and in the fines, and F1 separates that fraction mechanically before any chemistry. P-01 and P-02 both depend on this; it is the single most valuable thing to measure, and it is measurable.

**What I am not claiming, and the dissent I preserve.** The judge found F3 to have the clearest polymer-separation logic. I do not contest that as a statement about *polymer recovery*; I contest using it to select the *sink-design* starting point, because F3's solvent and acid inventory, its flammable-fluid regime and its unresolved recovery denominator make every sink iteration expensive, and because F3 leaves the metal class untouched by construction (P-02). If the stage's goal is polymer recovery rather than sink design, F3 is the better starting point and P-02 covers it. Slot B's specific contribution is that **the metal class, not nicotine, decides whether a recovered-CA product class is viable** — and only one family in the batch (F4, and only with a chelating extractant that its own design space admits) has a disclosed mechanism to move metals at all. **F2 is not a starting point**: no off-gas capture step is described anywhere in the patent while pollutants are stated to evaporate; the product is a 10–30 % minority filler in 70–90 % virgin thermoplastic, so any contaminant reduction in the product is arithmetic dilution; its operator's own LCA rejected the material route and the site closed; and the Korean comparator assayed the *finished product* and found PAHs and TSNAs. **F4 is not a starting point** for sink work: the operating point is unresolved (≈50/60 bar at ≈30 °C press quote versus a 280–320 bar / 15–80 °C patent envelope, with the patent's own CO₂ passage giving ≈31 °C and 73.85 bar), the company reports a 0.3 % concentrate whose denominator is unstated, and the class it moves least well without an extractant is exactly the class that decides the product gate.

**Which class is hardest, and what the route does about it.** Metals. Cd and Pb are non-volatile, do not follow water in a wash, and are carried by the inorganic/ash fraction that ends up in the CA. So the honest structure of my answer is: **P-01 solves the nicotine and (partially) the organic-TSNA/PAH problem by creating a characterisable concentrate, and deliberately leaves the metal problem unsolved in the product**; P-02 is the attempt to move metals into an acid phase; and if P-02's metal-partition assumption fails, the conclusion is that the CA-recovery product class is not viable for metals at all and the product class must change. I prefer this to a route that quietly lets metals ride into a consumer article.

**Waste classification and routing (the real constraint).** My fetch of the INERIS study page confirms the framing: "Les mégots de cigarettes sont à considérer, au sens de la réglementation déchets, comme des déchets dangereux." Two precision points the packet lacks, both from my searches: (i) INERIS's own 2018 committee report attributes HP6 to **calculation from nicotine content** and HP14 **only to ecotoxicological test results** — the two properties rest on different evidence types, and the peer-reviewed INERIS paper (Rebischung, Chabot, Biaudet & Pandard, *Waste Management* 82 (2018) 9–14, doi:10.1016/j.wasman.2018.09.038) says so explicitly: HP14 "was also determined but only by ecotoxicological tests results"; (ii) the INERIS study page I read names **no HP code at all**. So the HP6/HP14 attribution should be re-pointed to the 2018 committee report and the 2018 *Waste Management* paper, not to the current study page and not to the project's own summary note. Consequence for sink design: **removing nicotine can clear HP6; only a bio-assay on the material can clear HP14.** A chemical panel cannot substitute, which is why §2.4's product gate includes an ecotoxicity endpoint. INERIS also records (search excerpt) that nicotine content subjects the butt waste to dangerous-goods transport (TMD), that storage in waste-storage installations is excluded because COT exceeds acceptability thresholds, and that the 2017 report identified **two** output fractions — the compostable paper/tobacco/ash fraction, to be analysed and required to comply with NF U 44-051, and the plastic filter fraction, to be analysed and able to obtain **Sortie du Statut de Déchet (SSD)** through installations classées. The French reader's notes add UN code 2811 "Solide organique toxique, NSA" with packaging group III.

### Q3 — Measurement plan

**Feed basis.** One collected-butts lot from one declared collection stream, homogenised, moisture determined by a documented loss-on-drying method (the specific method and endpoint are **parameter-not-located** in the packet; De Fenzo's 60 °C/60 min drying is a source-reported *process* step, not a reference moisture method). Every mass in this plan is **dry mass** unless a stream is by nature aqueous, in which case both wet volume and dry mass are reported and the denominator is stated per stream.

**Aliquot design.** The dried, milled lot is split into equal-dry-mass aliquots, one per route, plus retained reference aliquots. **Replicate number is parameter-not-located** and must be set from a measured repeatability study, not assumed: the CORESTA 2006 Joint Experiment reported among-laboratory variability for regulatory purposes in the range of **35–45 % for benzo[a]pyrene and 26–55 % for TSNAs**. Any closure threshold tighter than the method's own reproducibility is therefore not measurable, and I propose none.

**Analyte panel and methods** (methods named from the CORESTA↔ISO correspondence list; these are methods for *tobacco/smoke* matrices, and their transfer to a butt-derived matrix requires validated extraction and matrix-effect checking — I am not claiming they work unmodified):

| Analyte | Candidate method (from CORESTA/ISO correspondence) |
|---|---|
| Nicotine | CRM 62 (GC, tobacco products) / ISO 22253; CRM 66 for cigar mainstream smoke |
| TSNAs | CRM 72 (LC-MS/MS, tobacco and tobacco products) / ISO 21766; CRM 75 (mainstream smoke LC-MS/MS) / ISO 19290 and ISO 23921 |
| PAHs | CRM 91 (15 PAHs in tobacco and tobacco products, GC-MS/MS or GC-MS); CRM 82 (benzo[a]pyrene in tobacco products, GC-MS); CRM 58 / ISO 22634-1 for benzo[a]pyrene in mainstream smoke |
| Cd, As | CRM 93 (cadmium and arsenic in tobacco products, ICP-MS) — and Pb/Hg by an ICP-MS method: **parameter-not-located** |
| Residual solvent (acetone, acetic acid, ethanol) | **parameter-not-located** — no CORESTA/ISO method identified for this matrix |
| COD | **parameter-not-located** as a method reference; the packet contains only the F5 value 29,986 mg/L (mg COD per litre of dark liquor) |
| Ecotoxicity (product gate only) | INERIS-type battery — aquatic and terrestrial species including algae, daphnia, lettuce, turnip, earthworm and bacteria (species list from the reader's notes on the 2017 report); the reported endpoints are *reproduction failure* and *growth inhibition*, not mortality alone: *Ceriodaphnia dubia* did not reproduce after 96 h in the presence of 0.06 % butt eluate, and micro-algal growth (*P. subcapitata*) was paralysed after 72 h at 0.77 % eluate (Le Monde, 2018, citing the INERIS report; secondary) |

**Streams measured.** Feed; product (recovered CA / pulp / composite); every liquid phase (first wash, later wash fractions, recycle, liquor mother liquor, precipitate washings, solvent condensate, purge); every solid (sludge/fines/ash, filtered residue, precipitate, mill fines, drying residue); captured off-gas/condensate wherever any thermal step is applied; the treatment outlet after the chosen train; and unexplained loss by difference.

**Closure and the decision rule.** For each analyte *i* and route *j*: `C_i,j = Σ M_i,outputs / M_i,feed` and `R_i,j = M_i,product / M_i,feed`, both on a dry-mass feed basis, reported with replicate scatter, spiked-recovery correction, blank corrections and the method's LOQ. **I propose no numeric pass/fail threshold**, for the reasons the packet and the judge both give, and for one further reason specific to this panel: the CORESTA tolerances above mean that a tight closure number would be a statement about laboratory scatter, not about chemistry. Instead the decision is a **three-way classification that uses no invented number**:
- **"unresolved"** if any stream's analyte mass is below its LOQ, or if `C` is not distinguishable from 1.0 within the combined uncertainty band;
- **"sink-confirmed"** if a *named* stream carries the analyte mass, that stream has a **collection boundary** and a **named final treatment**, and the product retention falls below the *use-specific* product criterion of §2.4 — the criterion being a standard, not a number I supply;
- **"retention"** if the product dominates.
A route that shows a large `1 − C` is an *emission or loss*, which is disqualifying for any clean-product claim — this is the one hard rule I do propose, and it is a rule about closure accounting rather than a threshold.

**Use-specific product gate (kept separate from the fate balance, per the judge's bounded revision 7).** The gate is the standard that governs the *intended use*, with the analytes that standard does not cover added explicitly:
- *Soil / compost use* → NF U 44-051 ("Amendements organiques — Dénominations, spécifications et marquage", AFNOR 2006, with amendments A1 2010, A2 2018, A3 2019 per the Legifrance homologation list), **plus** a TSNA panel and an ecotoxicity endpoint, because the tabulated criteria cover metals and three PAHs but not TSNAs, and because the AFNOR text itself was not obtained by me — the limit values I quote (Cd < 1.5, Pb < 150, Ni < 50, Cr < 100, Zn < 400, Cu < 100, Hg < 1, As < 18 mg/kg dry matter; fluoranthene < 4, benzo(b)fluoranthene < 4, benzo(a)pyrene < 2.5 mg/kg dry matter) come from a **secondary comparative review**, not the standard, and must be confirmed against it before use.
- *Non-food, non-skin-contact articles* (composite panels, furniture) → no product chemical standard applies, so the gate becomes an **exposure-bound gate**: measured product content, a declared use restriction, an end-of-life leaching scenario, and an SSD route for the material.
- *Skin-contact or toy uses* → the toy-migration regime applies (EN 71 series; the specific limits are **parameter-not-located** here — I did not retrieve them and will not quote numbers).
- *Food or drinking-water contact* → **no route proposed**; this is excluded by design, not by measurement, since I located no standard and no End-of-Waste determination for a butt-derived CA in any contact use.
- *Compost made from the tobacco/ash fraction* → NF U 44-051 plus the TSNA/ecotoxicity addition above, plus the requirement INERIS itself states for the compostable fraction.

### Q4 — Adversarial self-screening: the strongest objection to my own proposal

**The version that fails.** P-01's bleed is a *disposal route wearing a recycling costume*. Suppose the first-displacement liquor contains the nicotine at a concentration too low to trigger a hazardous-waste classification on its own, but a total toxicant mass so large that it cannot be discharged, landfilled (INERIS records storage as excluded because COT exceeds acceptability thresholds) or treated at a cost below the value of the recovered CA. Then P-01 has not created a sink; it has created **a large dilute liquid effluent with a tidy label and a paperwork advantage**, while simultaneously *increasing* water demand relative to F1's closed loop. That is precisely the structural objection that ended the Keenat material route on its own operator's accounting, and I would be repeating it. Worse: because the bleed fraction carries fines, the "sink" may be exporting recoverable CA, so the route could be economically irrational even if its chemistry is perfect. And the objector has a sharper version still: **the toxic mass is probably not in the filter fraction at all.** Nicotine, TSNAs and PAHs are combustion products of the tobacco; F1 separates tobacco/paper/ash mechanically *before* any chemistry, and that fraction is already the natural, low-value disposal route. If the ash fraction carries the larger share of the analyte mass, then P-01 is bleeding the wrong stream, and the *correct* design is much simpler: send the separated ash fraction to the sink (as a hazardous or as a controlled route), keep the filter wash loop closed with a small, measured, assayed purge, and apply the product gate to a much cleaner CA. That version has no large liquid stream at all and is strictly better. P-01 as I wrote it may be the wrong answer to a question I should have asked first.

**What would address it.** The single discriminating measurement is a **partition ranking on one feed**: for each of nicotine, TSNAs, PAHs and metals, the share of total feed analyte mass in (a) the separated tobacco/ash fraction, (b) the first wash liquor, (c) later wash fractions, (d) the CA product. This is a *ranking*, not a pass/fail, so it needs no invented threshold and no pass/fail number is implied. Two outcomes: if the ash fraction dominates, the architecture simplifies to "ash fraction = sink, closed loop with a measured purge" and P-01 should be replaced by that leaner version (I would withdraw the bleed and keep the loop); if the wash liquor dominates, P-01's bleed is aimed correctly and the remaining risk is route affordability, which is a permitting and cost question, not a chemistry question. Either way the measurement is the same, and the outcome is a design decision rather than a belief.

**A second, separate self-objection.** P-02's core assumption — that metals partition to the acid phase — is untested and, on the packet's own chemistry, genuinely doubtful for Cd and Pb. If it fails, then no route in this batch produces a metals-compliant CA and the CA-recovery product class is dead for reasons unrelated to process maturity. I state this as a possible outcome rather than defending the assumption. And note the meta-point the question raises: **P-01, P-02 and P-03 are new hypotheses I created at Stage 4 by combining elements of different families. None is a validated process, none has been operated, and none should be read as one.** The strongest thing I can say for them is that each has a measurement that could kill it, and that the measurements are ones the batch has already agreed it needs.

Two consequences for Stage 4: (a) the comparator assayed the **finished product**, so it is a *product-gate* comparator, not a process comparator — which is exactly the gate I propose in §2.4; (b) the authors' statement is a constraint on *claims*, not a proof that no route can work, and it should be read as "the product must be assayed and bounded", not "detoxification is impossible".
5. **The "use in this lab" instruction in the Korean note is a verdict rule where a measurement rule is needed.** It says any spec without a toxin-removal mechanism that survives this question "fails phase 3". I dissent. The question a route must survive is not "did you remove the toxin" but "does the toxic fraction have a characterisable, lawful sink, and is the product's use bounded by measured content". A route with no removal mechanism but a lawful sink and a use-restricted, assayed product is not thereby invalid. My Q1/Q2 answers rest on this dissent, and the judge's bounded revision #4 (comparators should trigger measurements rather than verdicts) supports it.
6. **The Keenat-LCA note's "use in this lab" rule is inapplicable to every family, including F2.** It says a method that "only moves toxins around or costs more energy than it saves must be marked as failing this benchmark". The batch contains **no** energy, cost or LCA data for any family (G-16), so the rule cannot be applied to F2 *or* to any other route — and certainly not to a proposal of mine, since I have no energy denominators either. I preserve the dissent (the operator's own retreat is material evidence about F2 specifically) but I decline to apply it as a cross-family filter.
7. **The packet's NF U 44-051 requirement, once made precise, is weaker than the packet's framing implies — and weaker in the specific way that matters for slot B.** INERIS requires the compostable fraction to be analysed and to comply with NF U 44-051. But the tabulated NF U 44-051 criteria (secondary tabulation; AFNOR text not obtained) cover metals and three PAHs and **contain no TSNA criterion at all**, while permitting benzo[a]pyrene at ~2.5 mg/kg dry matter and fluoranthene at ~4 mg/kg dry matter. So "compost complies with NF U 44-051" is **not** a detoxification demonstration, and it does not touch one of the two carcinogen classes the Korean comparator found. Any Stage-5 sink that treats compost compliance as sufficient should be corrected.
8. **The Stage-4 question calls the Keenat and Korean notes "secondary comparators, not family verdicts" — agreed, and I would go one step further.** Both comparators are *product-side* evidence. Neither is evidence about process chemistry, and neither bears on a route that does not put the material into a matrix. The comparator that actually discriminates sink design is INERIS's own output-fraction logic, which is about *where each fraction goes* and *under what criterion*, not about which process is better.
