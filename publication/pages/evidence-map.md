# From sources to recycling methods

Start with a reported route, then look for measurements that could change how you judge it. This is a selection of examples, not a list of every company, patent or laboratory study.

## Separate and wash the filter

**What is reported.** [De Fenzo and colleagues](/sources/defenzo-2020-materials/) washed spent filters with water and ethanol and characterised the recovered polymer. [MéGo!'s patent publication](/sources/mego-fr3066128a1/) describes a mechanical separation and washing route. These are distinct disclosures; the recovered material's properties do not by themselves establish what was removed.

**AI hypothesis.** A wash stream could carry both dissolved contaminants and loose filter fibres. [Belzagui and colleagues](/sources/belzagui-2021-microfibres/) observed fibre release in water, but not in an industrial wash. Checking solids and liquid separately could reveal transfers that a dissolved-only analysis would miss. Compare the two fractions and the treated fibre against the same input. [Read the washing family](/methods/mechanical-physical/).

## Recover the polymer or turn it into pulp

**What is reported.** The [CNR patent offering](/sources/cnr-knowledge-share-ca-recovery/) describes dissolving and recovering cellulose acetate. [Teixeira and colleagues](/sources/teixeira-2017-pulp/) instead reported making cellulose pulp. Their pulping study describes a dark liquor with metals and organic residues. Its treatment did not meet the study's target for chemical oxygen demand; the authors also reported reusing clarified liquor in their trials.

**AI hypothesis.** Reuse can defer a discharge without eliminating the residues. A stable mass balance across repeated runs, including removed solids and any purge, would distinguish genuine control from build-up. Compare recovered acetate and pulp against different use specifications: they are not the same product. [Read the chemical family](/methods/chemical/).

## Make a composite or extract before making insulation

**What is reported.** [Keenat](/sources/keenat-dry-thermal/) describes a dry composite route. [TerraCycle's handout](/sources/terracycle-process/) describes separating paper and tobacco from the filter stream and blending pelletised filter material with other plastics. A [French patent document](/sources/tchaomegot-fr3102076b3/) describes a different approach: extracting residues with supercritical fluid before making insulation.

**Still open.** Company descriptions and patent disclosures document what is claimed or described, not independent results. A technical comparison needs worker-exposure evidence, product release under wear, and a destination for the separated residues or extract. It does not require guessing private operating settings. [Read the dry-treatment family](/methods/thermal/).

**A permit is a different kind of evidence.** The [Trento provincial authorisation for Re-Cig](/sources/recig-trento-permit/) documents a specified site and a route from washing to granulation. Its annex lists lot conformity criteria. These criteria do not establish measured metals removal or acceptance of a different plant or product.

## Put butt-derived fibre into construction materials

**What is reported.** [Rahman and colleagues](/sources/rahman-2020-bitumen/) tested butt fibre as a binder modifier; [Jin and colleagues](/sources/jin-2019-asphalt/) compared asphalt mixtures. A [fired-clay laboratory study](/sources/kurmus-2020-bricks/) is a separate construction route. Binder behaviour, road-mixture performance and brick properties are different endpoints.

**AI hypothesis.** A matrix that meets an initial performance test may still release material after ageing. A comparison against unmodified material, under the same wear or weathering conditions, could show whether the addition changes release. Keep manufacturing emissions separate from release during use and after demolition. [Read the construction family](/methods/material-incorporation/).

## Biological treatment and other possible outputs

- **Biological treatment:** The [biological family](/methods/biological/) separates the reported treatment-device lead from actual evidence of degradation. A change in appearance or mass is not necessarily polymer breakdown or contaminant removal.
- **Non-patent company leads:** [Code Effort's public knowledge page](/sources/code-effort-knowledge-centre/) offers consultation; [trade reporting](/sources/code-effort-coverage/) describes uses for fibre, paper and tobacco. Neither reference is a complete verified operating protocol. Inclusion here does not depend on holding, or on the absence of, a patent.
- **Lab prototypes:** Butt-derived fibre has been investigated for [air filtration](/sources/air-filtration-2026/) and as a [support for a dye-treatment catalyst](/sources/dye-photocatalyst-2024/). A performance test for a prototype is not evidence for medical, food-contact, or consumer use. [See candidate products](/methods/product-routes/).

## Supporting research changes what to check

- [Marinello et al.](/sources/marinello-2020-review/) and [Torkashvand & Farzadkia](/sources/torkashvand-2019-review/) map routes and point to primary studies; they are not extra independent tests of those routes.
- The [Aizawl litter study](/sources/nongdren-2024-leachate/) estimates water-pollution and exposure potential in a specific setting. It does not establish exposure from a recycled product.
- The [end-of-life life-cycle study](/sources/nongdren-2026-lca/) compares modelled scenarios. Its results depend on the assumptions about collection, energy, displaced materials and emissions. It does not validate the impact of any individual processor.
- The [2017 Ineris report](/sources/ineris-2017-collection-treatment/) gives chemical and leachate results for its samples (§6); its [2019 follow-up](/sources/ineris-2019-recycling/) surveys management routes. Neither provides current clearance for a new site.
- [Générations sans Tabac](/sources/gst-2025-critique/) offers a critical public-health perspective. Use it to check what recycling claims leave out, not as a substitute for measurements.

This page was produced by desk research. It does not authorise any experiment.
