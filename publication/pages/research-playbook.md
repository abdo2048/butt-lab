# A repeatable guide for researching any waste material

Use this for a single material and one practical question at a time. You do not need multiple AI agents, rounds of voting, or a finished worldwide inventory before you can publish a useful first version.

## 1. Define the material and decision

What exactly arrives: a clean part, a used product, or mixed waste? Record common variations, current disposal routes, local infrastructure and plausible hazards. Pick an intended output and a baseline to compare it with. Keep local constraints separate from claims that are true only for a different place or feedstock.

## 2. Build one source list

Start from a review or an [existing recycling directory](https://abdo2048.github.io/obsidian-quartz-template/), then follow citations to papers, patents, theses, company pages, permits, independent tests and negative results. Record the title, URL, version, page or section, and what you actually read. Label entries as lead, screened or used for a particular claim. A translated or OCR copy is a reading aid; check important values against the original. Group duplicate formats under one source. Do not copy documents into a public site without clearance.

## 3. Write a card for each route

- **Input and output:** What goes in, what changes, and what comes out?
- **Reported:** What did the authors say or measure? Include the locator, units and denominator. Distinguish a disclosure from a plant result.
- **Still unknown:** Which step, outcome or waste destination is unsupported? Keep unknown inputs blank rather than filling them with industry averages.
- **AI hypothesis:** Offer one plausible explanation or design alternative. Name the source that inspired it, what must be true, and the observation that would prove it wrong.
- **Practical constraints:** What facility, testing, receiving outlet, or intended-use standard would a qualified reviewer check? If no outlet or measurement is known, say so.

## 4. Compare and propose

Compare routes that serve the same purpose, on the same basis. Keep yield, material quality, human exposure, by-products and resource burden separate. Propose a candidate improvement only after showing what it borrows and where it differs. Use simple scenario calculations to decide what to measure first; publish the equations, input assumptions and results. A scenario is not a trial or a prediction. If a ranking flips when an unknown changes, label the decision unresolved.

## 5. Publish, review and revise

Publish only traceable claims and explicitly labelled ideas. Invite reviewers to submit the exact page and statement, a source locator or dataset, and the change they recommend. For actual lab or field work, a qualified team must establish the risk assessment, acceptance criteria, controls and disposal arrangements before any trial. Record what happened, including failures and deviations, without overwriting prior evidence states. Then update only the claims the new evidence changes. Stop a desk pass when the next decision needs a measurement or a specific missing source; leave that as the next task, not an indefinite search.

This guide does not authorise any experiment.
