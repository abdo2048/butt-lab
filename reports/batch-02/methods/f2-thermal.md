# Method Family F2 — Dry Treatment / Extrusion Composite

## Overview
F2 is a dry mechanical treatment process in which cigarette butts are processed without water or solvent washing, then combined with a binder matrix (typically a thermoplastic such as polyethylene or polypropylene) and extruded into composite profiles. The Keenat process is the primary reference for this family: cigarette butts are collected, sorted, possibly milled, then blended with recycled plastic and extruded into products such as street furniture (benches, bollards, planters). No washing stage is used; contaminants remain bound to the butt material and are encapsulated within the composite matrix.

## Mechanism
1. **Collection and sorting**: Spent cigarette butts are collected and sorted to remove non-butt debris.
2. **Drying**: Butts are dried to remove moisture (critical before extrusion to avoid steam voids and degradation).
3. **Size reduction**: Butts are milled or ground to a fibrous/particulate form suitable for blending with plastic.
4. **Blending**: Milled butts are mixed with a thermoplastic resin (e.g., recycled polyethylene or polypropylene), sometimes with additives (coupling agents, UV stabilizers, colorants).
5. **Extrusion**: The blend is fed into an extruder, melted, and formed into profiles (planks, posts, panels).
6. **Cooling and cutting**: Extruded profiles are cooled and cut to length.

The cellulose acetate filter and paper remain physically embedded in the plastic matrix; contaminants (nicotine, tar, heavy metals) are not removed but are physically contained.
