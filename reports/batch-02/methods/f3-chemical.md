# Method Family F3 — Chemical / Solvent Dissolution

## Overview
F3 uses chemical solvents to dissolve the cellulose acetate filter material from cigarette butts, separating it from paper, tobacco remnants, and contaminants. The dissolved cellulose acetate is then precipitated (typically by adding a non-solvent such as water), washed, dried, and recovered as a purified cellulose-acetate product. Solvents reported in the literature include acetone, N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP), dimethylacetamide (DMAc), and tetrahydrofuran (THF). F3 is positioned as the highest-purity recovery route: it targets the valuable cellulose-acetate polymer itself rather than using butts as filler.

## Mechanism
1. **Collection and preparation**: Spent cigarette butts are collected; tobacco and paper may be separated or left in depending on the protocol.
2. **Dissolution**: Butts are immersed in a solvent that dissolves cellulose acetate (e.g., acetone, NMP, DMAc, THF) at controlled temperature, sometimes with agitation.
3. **Filtration**: Undissolved residues (paper, tobacco, ash, some contaminants) are filtered out.
4. **Precipitation**: The cellulose-acetate solution is added to a non-solvent (typically water), causing the polymer to precipitate as a solid.
5. **Washing**: The precipitated solid is washed to remove residual solvent and dissolved contaminants.
6. **Drying**: The washed solid is dried to recover cellulose acetate.
7. **Solvent recovery**: The solvent/non-solvent mixture may be distilled and recycled (claimed by some processes, unmeasured).

The goal is to recover relatively pure cellulose acetate, a thermoplastic polymer used in textiles, films, membranes, and other applications.

## Contaminant Fate
- **Nicotine and tar components**: Partially removed if they remain in the filtration residue or are washed out during precipitation washing; some may co-dissolve with cellulose acetate and remain in the product.
- **Heavy metals (cadmium, lead, arsenic)**: May remain in filtration residue or may co-dissolve; removal efficiency unknown.
- **Solvent residues**: Traces of solvent may remain in recovered cellulose acetate unless washing is thorough.
- **Spent solvent/non-solvent mixture**: Contaminated with dissolved toxins; requires hazardous-waste handling and treatment, not disposal to drain.
- **Filtration residue**: Contains paper, tobacco, ash, and some contaminants; requires disposal as contaminated solid waste.

## Regulatory Status
- **Waste classification**: Spent cigarette butts may be classified as hazardous waste; spent solvent contaminated with extracted toxins requires hazardous-waste handling.
- **Chemical regulations**: Solvents (acetone, NMP, DMAc, THF) are regulated chemicals with handling, storage, and disposal requirements; NMP is a reproductive toxicant under REACH.
- **Product contact**: Recovered cellulose acetate is not approved for food contact, skin contact, or any consumer product application.
- **End-of-waste**: No jurisdiction is known to grant end-of-waste status to solvent-recovered cellulose acetate from cigarette butts.
- **Wastewater**: Spent wash liquor containing extracted toxins and solvent residues requires treatment as contaminated effluent.
