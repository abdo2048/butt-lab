# Method Family F4 — Biological

## Overview
F4 uses biological agents—enzymes, fungi, bacteria, or microbial consortia—to degrade or transform components of cigarette butts. The cellulose acetate filter is a synthetic polymer resistant to natural biodegradation; some research has explored enzyme-mediated deacetylation (converting cellulose acetate back toward cellulose) or microbial degradation of tobacco and paper components. F4 is at the earliest stage of development among the method families: no commercial process, no pilot-scale demonstration, and no published evidence of full-butt biological recycling at any scale.

## Mechanism
1. **Collection and preparation**: Spent cigarette butts are collected and possibly pre-treated (shredded, dried, or sterilized).
2. **Biological treatment**: Butts are exposed to enzymes (e.g., esterases, deacetylases) or microorganisms (fungi, bacteria) under controlled conditions (temperature, pH, moisture, aeration).
3. **Incubation**: The biological agents act over hours to days, potentially deacetylating cellulose acetate or degrading paper/tobacco components.
4. **Separation**: Treated material is separated from the biological medium (filtration, centrifugation).
5. **Recovery**: Any recovered cellulose or cellulose-acetate derivative is washed and dried.
6. **Residual handling**: Spent biological medium containing degraded components requires disposal.

The goal is to break down cigarette-butt components into simpler, more manageable materials—or to modify cellulose acetate into a more biodegradable form.

## Contaminant Fate
- **Nicotine and tar components**: May be degraded by some microorganisms; degradation efficiency and products unknown.
- **Heavy metals (cadmium, lead, arsenic)**: Not destroyed by biological processes; may be bioaccumulated by microorganisms or remain in treated material/spent medium.
- **Cellulose acetate**: May be partially deacetylated or degraded; extent unknown; no evidence of complete mineralization.
- **Paper and tobacco**: More readily biodegradable than cellulose acetate; may be partially degraded.
- **Spent biological medium**: Contains degraded components, nutrients, and possibly bioaccumulated metals; requires disposal as contaminated effluent.
- **Microplastics**: Partial deacetylation does not eliminate microplastic potential; complete mineralization is unproven.

## Bottom Line
F4 biological treatment is the least-developed method family: no commercial process, no pilot-scale demonstration, and no published evidence that cigarette butts can be biologically recycled at any scale. Cellulose acetate is notoriously resistant to biodegradation; nicotine, tar components, and heavy metals fate is unknown; no product acceptance criteria exist. F4 remains a research hypothesis, not a demonstrated method.
