# Method Family F6 — Product Routes

## Overview
F6 is not a processing method itself; it is a survey of the end-product pathways into which recycled cigarette-butt material could be routed. Different method families (F0–F5) produce different intermediate outputs (washed fiber, milled particles, recovered polymer, deacetylated cellulose, incorporated composite), and each intermediate could feed different product routes. F6 maps those routes: construction materials, composite products, textiles, films/membranes, insulation, and energy recovery — with the same guardrails applied: no clean/safe/validated claims, no experiment, metals stop-condition, four-field sink required.

## Product Routes

### 6.1 Construction Materials (bricks, concrete, tiles, asphalt)
Butt material (milled particles or ash) incorporated as partial aggregate or fiber replacement in clay bricks, concrete, tiles, or asphalt mixes.
- **Claimed benefit**: Partial replacement of virgin aggregate or cement; diversion of butts from waste stream.
- **Known issues**: Contaminants (nicotine, tar, heavy metals) remain in product; leaching potential unmeasured; firing emissions (kiln) may release volatiles; mechanical properties untested at scale; no building-code acceptance.
- **Evidence**: Small-scale academic studies on butts in bricks/concrete; no commercial-scale demonstration.
- **Status**: Concept only; no product standard, no regulatory pathway.

### 6.2 Composite Profiles (extruded planks, posts, panels)
Butt particles blended with thermoplastic resin (PE/PP) and extruded into outdoor furniture, bollards, decking, or construction profiles (F2 route).
- **Claimed benefit**: Plastic-wood composite alternative using butts as filler.
- **Known issues**: Contaminants encapsulated, not removed; leaching/weathering unknown; end-of-life disposal undefined; mechanical properties unmeasured; no product standard.
- **Evidence**: Keenat company claims; no independent performance or LCA data.
- **Status**: Concept/pilot claims only; no independent verification.

### 6.3 Textiles and Nonwovens
Recovered cellulose acetate (F3 solvent dissolution route) or deacetylated cellulose (F4 biological route) spun into fibers for textiles or nonwoven products.
- **Claimed benefit**: Bio-based or recycled fiber alternative.
- **Known issues**: Recovered polymer purity unknown; residual solvent or nicotine may remain; no textile standard accepts it; skin-contact safety unproven.
- **Evidence**: Patent literature on cellulose acetate dissolution; no cigarette-butt-specific textile studies.
- **Status**: Concept only; no product standard, no safety data.

### 6.4 Films and Membranes
Recovered cellulose acetate cast into films or membranes for packaging, filtration, or separation applications.
- **Claimed benefit**: Biodegradable or recyclable film alternative.
- **Known issues**: Same as textiles — purity, residual contaminants, safety, no standard acceptance.
- **Evidence**: General cellulose-acetate film literature; no cigarette-butt-specific studies.
- **Status**: Concept only.

### 6.5 Insulation and Panels
Butt fiber or composite material used in insulation boards or acoustic panels.
- **Claimed benefit**: Insulation value from cellulose content; waste diversion.
- **Known issues**: Fire performance unknown (cellulose acetate is flammable); contaminant release during installation or use; no building-code acceptance.
- **Evidence**: None specific to cigarette-butt insulation.
- **Status**: Concept only.

### 6.6 Energy Recovery (incineration with energy capture)
Butts used as fuel in waste-to-energy incineration.
- **Claimed benefit**: Energy recovery from waste; volume reduction.
- **Known issues**: Emissions from burning butts (nicotine, tar, heavy metals in flue gas) require air-pollution controls; ash contains concentrated toxins; not "recycling" in the material-recovery sense.
- **Evidence**: None specific to cigarette-butt incineration.
- **Status**: Concept only; air-quality permits would be required.

## Bottom Line
F6 maps six potential product routes for recycled cigarette-butt material. None have independent evidence of safety, performance, or environmental benefit. All routes retain contaminants unless paired with a contaminant-removal method (F0 washing, F3 dissolution, or F4 deacetylation). No product standard, building code, or regulatory pathway accepts cigarette-butt material for any product. All routes remain unvalidated hypotheses pending analytic measurement.

**Metals stop-condition**: Heavy metal content of any candidate product and any leaching potential must be measured before consideration; if metals exceed safe thresholds or leach, the route stops.
