Abstract
Cigarette butt (CB) is a crucial litter in urban communities because it may contain various toxicants. Due to serious
limitations on incinerating or landfilling CB, recycling of this hazardous waste is essential. The objective of this
study was to investigate the most important challenges in CB collection and recycling. To this end, a search was
done on Scopus, PubMed, and Web of Science by defining a search protocol and identifying appropriate keywords.
At the end of the screening process, 52 appropriate papers were selected. In this review, all methods for the CB
recycling were considered. This review showed that nine categorizations of different products have been produced
from the recycling of CBs, but three important challenges were identified for the recycling of this hazardous waste.
It should be noted that finding solutions to these challenges may be helpful in better management of CB as a toxic
litter.
Keywords CB littering . CB contaminants . Waste management

Introduction
Cigarette butt (CB) is a waste consisting cigarette filter,
wrapping paper, and remained tobacco after smoking
(Barnes 2011; Hamzah and Umar 2017; Novotny et al.
2009). It is claimed that 4.5 trillion CBs are being produced annually across the globe, weighing 0.8 to 1.2 million tons, and it is expected to increase by 50% in 2025
(Kadir and Mohajerani 2012; Kadir and Mohajerani 2011;
Mohajerani et al. 2017; Ou et al. 2016; Yazdi et al.
2012). A cigarette filter is designed to trap the contaminants of cigarette smoke (Mohajerani et al. 2017); thus, it
contains various contaminants originated from cigarette
smoke. According to the literature, some metals and pollutants such as polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)

Another challenge of CB recycling is environmental concerns because of the emission of CB pollutants in recycling
processes, as well as the presence of pollution in final products. The use of chemicals even water in recycling stages with
the aim of the cleaned filter in recycling for the production of
some products such as activated carbon, selective adsorbent,
and hydrophobic fibers has been reported (Hamzah and Umar
2017; Li et al. 2019; Ou et al. 2016).
During this process, the contaminants are leached out from
CB and transferred into the liquid phase, ultimately leading to
the production of a highly polluted leachate. Teixeira et al.
(2017) reported that the presence of trapped contaminates in
CB has led to the production of a strong dark liquor with high
chemical oxygen demand (COD) concentration during deriving cellulose acetate fiber from CB (Teixeira et al. 2017).
Therefore, the production of polluted effluent is a serious
problem in CB recycling, which needs treatment and management of the pollution. It should be pointed out that some contaminants determined in CBs like heavy metals and PAHs are
dangerous for the environment and ecosystems (Mansouri
et al. 2020b, a, Torkashvand et al. 2021b). Thus, some
methods are needed to treat the pollutants trapped in CBs.
For example, thin films of cellulose acetate nanofibers from
CB can be named (Arroyo et al. 2020).
Since the toxicity and adverse effects of chemicals leaked
from CBs in the environment have been confirmed (Green
et al. 2020; Montalvão et al. 2019; Parker and Rayburn
2017) the prevention from the emission of these pollutants
in recycling steps is of great importance. However, trapped
pollutants may be emitted during the heat-processing of this
litter, especially for building material products such as bricks
and asphalt from a mixture of CBs and raw material. Besides,
the emission of gaseous pollutants during some CBs recycling
processes is another important concern. Mohajerani et al.

In methods of CB utilization for making new products, the
effect of CBs pollution level on the quality of the final product
will be significant. The quality and structure of CBs determines the final product quality or change some of the important final product characteristics. For example, by initial
mixing of raw material with CBs for brick preparation, the
density and heat conduction of the final product may be significantly declined, which can be advantageous (Kadir and
Mohajerani 2011; Kadir et al. 2015; Mohajerani et al. 2016;
Sarani 2013). However, Kadir and Mohajerani (2011) reported that the stability of bricks prepared from the initial mixture
containing 10% CB was decreased to 88%. Moreover, the
water absorption capacity of the bricks was increased by increasing the proportion of CB in initial mixture (Kadir and
Mohajerani 2011). Another study confirmed that the stability
of asphalt decreased with increasing the ratio of CB in the
composition of the initial materials (Sutantio et al. 2021).
Furthermore, a lower performance of final products in some
cases can be considered a disadvantage in CB recycling as it
has been reported that the sorption capacity of produced
aerogel declined after the initial usage (Uebe et al. 2021).
Regarding CB recycling methods, it seems that different
parts of this litter have various applications and can be converted into useful materials. However, in most studies, only
one aspect of these capabilities are taken into consideration
and an attempt to find a way to use the full capacity of CBs for
recycling can be very useful. In addition, the number of researches on the CB recycling methods is very limited, and one
of the challenges for the practical recycling of CB is limited
research-based information on the various aspects of CB
recycling methods. With respect to the information presented
in literature, significant efforts should be paid to the design of
integrated procedures for (i) proper CB disposal by smokers,
(ii) efficient CB collection, (iii) CB recycling, and (iv) controlling CB by-products during the recycling process in future.
Overall, there is a need for developing environmentally friendly and sustainable CB recycling programs.

characterize the problems and challenges in the recycling of
CB, which is one of the most abundant urban litters. Based on
a review on appropriate papers, it was found that the most
important issue in managing and recycling of CBs is the trouble with the inefficient collection of CB which dispersed in the
environment, and the most important task to overcome this
problem is trying to change the smokers’ behavior in the CB
