## Plain-language mechanism
Unlike keeping the original tow intact, this method pretreats the dirty filter material and then dissolves/reconstitutes acetate material using acetic acid. Separated polymer is washed and dried. The claimed high recovery is a polymer-recovery claim, not a percentage of contaminants removed.

The abstract's "only acetic acid" language describes the dissolution solvent. The complete workflow also uses ethanol and deionized water. It is not a solvent-free or water-free process.

## Embodiments: do not mix conditions from different examples
- Example 1: 50 mL glacial acetic acid, 95 C, 4 hours.
- Example 2: same reported volume/temperature, 0.25 hour.
- Example 3: 30 mL glacial acetic acid, 95 C, 4 hours.
- Example 4: 30 mL of 8 mol/L acetic acid, 95 C, 4 hours.
- Example 5: 50 mL glacial acetic acid, room temperature, 4 hours.
The examples share post-separation washing/aging/drying language. These are different examples, not five consecutive baths. Do not insert claim 4's preferred point into every example or assume glacial acid and 12 mol/L acid are interchangeable.

## What the source supports and does not support
Source assertions: acetate recovery, comparisons using XRD/IR/thermal characterization, claimed process optimization and recovery. This project has neither independently repeated those results nor extracted numerical figure datasets for fitting.

Not established here: nicotine/PAH/TSNA clearance, metal removal and leaching, residual acid/ethanol, solvent-loop stability, reliable strength/viscosity/molecular-weight retention, emissions, industrial economics, or approved end-product use. A 92% recovery statement must never become "92% detoxification" or "92% purity".

## Gaps requiring resolution before simulation or experiments
1. Exact input mass and normalization basis of the examples and recovery calculation.
2. Full stream accounting: ethanol extracts, aqueous washes, hot-separation residue, polymer-containing filtrate, final polymer, and vapors.
3. Clear distinction between removing undissolved residue and collecting precipitated polymer. The translated solid/liquid sequence needs independent technical reading; do not invent a fully specified antisolvent addition from implication alone.
4. Water use implied by the conductivity endpoint, and whether dissolved neutral organics remain when that endpoint is reached.
5. Quantitative product-quality and toxicology data beyond polymer identity.
6. Solvent recovery, contaminant accumulation, drying safeguards, energy, equipment compatibility, and hazardous-residue handling.
7. Granted B-version text and official legal status if later needed. No legal exclusion is applied to research collection.

## Engineering interpretation and next work
Hypothesis: this is a candidate polymer-dissolution/recovery route worth comparing to fiber-preserving washing, not proof it wins. Prioritize recovery-denominator clarification and contaminant partitioning before estimating per-tonne costs or buying equipment. Total pretreatment/drying time must be considered; the preferred two-hour dissolution is not an end-to-end two-hour process.
