# Company workflow dossiers (batch-01) — non-patent and partially patented operators

Date: 2026-11-03. Stage 2 / unit S2-01 companion (dossier v1.0). Public-source only. **Not an operating procedure or safety approval.** Company statements are self-report; none is an independent assay. No company was contacted. Reading limits are stated per entity.

Scope: Code Effort, TerraCycle, BuffIndia, Re-Cig, CNR/AzzeroCO2, Cerdia, with MéGo!, Poiato Recicla and the patent-only actors cross-referenced to their own specs. The purpose is to keep company workflows in the evidence base **without** converting marketing into process data.

## 2. Code Effort (India)

**Workflow (news-reported, company-described):**
1. Collection in "V bins" at shops/offices/smoking areas, plus ragpicker/contractor network; INR 200–400/kg paid (one source 500–800).
2. Transport to Noida facility; manual separation of fibre, paper, tobacco/ash.
3. Cutting/shredding in a custom machine; "less than 30 seconds per batch".
4. Fibre: soak in water, grind, treat with biodegradable chemicals 24–36 h, dry ~1 h, soften/open.
5. Paper: pulp, blend with essential oils (lemon-eucalyptus, citronella, picaridin) and organic binder → sheets/mats.
6. Tobacco/ash: aerobic decay ~30 days with bacteria/fungus → compost powder for nurseries.
7. Fibre used as stuffing/filling; some sold to textile mills.

**Scale claims:** 1,000 kg/day ≈ 3.5 M butts/day; 5 kg fibre/batch; up to 3 t/day fibre; plan 30→300 t/month by 2025; cumulative 300 M / 1.2 B / 12 B (inconsistent — see H01 §5).

**Certification claims:** ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2015, ISO 45001:2018; in-house lab QC of stuffing. No certificate number, lab or analyte list located.

**Why it matters for the panel:** it is the largest-volume documented operator in the batch and it uses an aqueous chemical wash that is *not* described in any patent. The undisclosed chemistry and the closed-loop/wastewater claim are the central evidence gap. A "biodegradable chemical" can still be a hazard (e.g. surfactant, alkali, oxidant) and "reused wastewater" can concentrate contaminants.

**Falsifiers:** fibre/product analyte data; wastewater volume/quality and reuse cycles; material safety data sheets for the chemicals; certificate numbers.

## 3. TerraCycle (US/Canada)

**Official workflow (company PDF, 3 steps):**
1. Collection: participants fill boxes; prepaid shipping to TerraCycle; aggregation in a local warehouse.
2. Processing: tobacco and paper dried and mixed with other organic material to make compost; filters (cellulose acetate) dried and turned into powder.
3. Conversion: CA powder used in recycled plastic lumber (benches, picnic tables, fencing, decks, playground equipment).

**What is absent:** no sanitisation or washing step in this PDF; no chemical names; no temperatures; no mass balance; no location or partner named; no assay data. The earlier project claim of a "shred + sanitize" trade secret is **not** corroborated by this document and remains `UNRESOLVED`.

**Why it matters:** TerraCycle is the best-known collector brand, and its public process is essentially **drying + powdering + downcycling into lumber**, i.e. encapsulation, not detoxification. This is the same product logic that the Korean paving-stone programme used and that Keenat's LCA rejected. It is a legitimate comparator but a weak detoxification claim.

**Falsifiers:** compost and lumber contaminant/leachate data; the identity of the processing partner; any permit or End-of-Waste determination.

## 4. BuffIndia (India)

**Workflow:** fire-proof kiosks on corporate/hotel premises (annual rental) + door-to-door collection → central Ahmedabad facility → "ISO-compliant process involving acidic hydrolysis, high-temperature treatment, and medical-grade testing" → recycled material "often combined with clay" → decorative/gifting products.

**Scale:** 1.8–2.5 t/month; ~3.2 M butts in March 2025; 960 kg "microplastic" upcycled to date; ~100 staff; 22–26 states/85 cities.

**Why it matters:** "acidic hydrolysis" is chemically in the same family as S2-03 (acid dissolution) and S2-05 (hydrolysis), but no patent or paper describes it. If the acid hydrolyses acetate to cellulose, the product is no longer CA; if it only protonates/solubilises, it may be a CA recovery route. One sentence is not enough to classify it. The clay composite introduces a mineral matrix whose leachate behaviour is unstudied.

**Falsifiers:** the acid identity and conditions; what "medical-grade testing" measured; product leachate; facility effluent permit.

## 5. Re-Cig (Italy)

**Workflow (company 4 steps):**
1. Sieve: remove ash, tobacco residue, debris.
2. Washing: **double wash cycle at variable temperatures** to separate paper from filter and "eliminate harmful substances"; **process water is entrusted to a certified partner for purification**.
3. Drying and "thermoplastification": remove moisture, hot mixing at controlled temperature, granulate.
4. End-of-Waste: declaration of conformity; material tested and released as Re-CA.

**Mass balance (company, university cases):** 15.50 kg butts → 6.00 kg polymer; 6.12 kg → 2.39 kg. `[CALCULATION]` ≈ 38.7 % and 39.1 % polymer yield on collected mass. No moisture, reject or wastewater load is given, so the yield denominator is not a dry-mass basis.

**Why it matters:** it is the most transparent industrial water-wash disclosure in the batch, because it admits an **external wastewater stream** rather than claiming a closed loop. That makes it the honest comparator for MéGo!'s closed-loop claim. The ~39 % yield is the only operator mass balance in the batch and should be used as an attributed figure, not as a literature constant.

**Falsifiers:** patent text (number not located); polymer purity and residual contaminant data; the certified partner's effluent data; End-of-Waste criteria.

## 6. CNR / AzzeroCO2 (Italy)

**Workflow (offering page):**
(a) soak filters/butts in acetone — solubilises CA and nicotine; (b) filter out paper, ash, tobacco residue; (c) add water as antisolvent to precipitate CA; (d) recover acetone by boiling for a new cycle; (e) treat/dispose the concentrated pollutant water.

Claims: no prior separation/pre-treatment; "clean" CA because pollutants remain in the acetone/water mixture; simple, cheap, low environmental impact; TRL 5 self-assessed; applications frames, masks, shields, strings.

**Why it matters:** chemically this is the cleanest separation concept in the batch — dissolution should reject insoluble paper/ash and leave organics in the solvent. The questions are engineering: acetone is flammable and volatile; water antisolvent creates a large dilute acetone/water stream; boiling to recover acetone consumes energy; nicotine and metals concentrate in the aqueous phase and need a hazardous-waste route. "Low environmental impact" is a claim, not an LCA.

**Falsifiers:** residual acetone in product; CA molecular weight/purity; energy for acetone recovery; wastewater nicotine/metals load and disposal cost; whether the offering and the CNR mask patent are one family.

## 7. Cerdia (Germany)

**Abstract-level only:** mechanical pretreatment to remove/separate foreign substances → cleaning with a cleaning agent so remaining impurities are washed off/out or extracted → optionally recover the cleaning agent, in particular by distillation. Covers post-consumer mouthpieces/filters, including heat-not-burn products.

**Why it matters:** an incumbent acetate-tow manufacturer filing on post-consumer CA recovery signals that recovered CA quality may be industrially relevant. But the cleaning agent is not named in the abstract read, and no company process or plant data was located. Treat as a **patent signal**, not a process.

**Falsifiers:** full description and claims; CA quality versus virgin tow; HT-product-specific contaminants (e.g. glycerol/aerosol residues).

## 8. Cross-entity observations for Stage 3

1. **Three product logics, not one.** (i) Recover CA and re-melt (MéGo!, Re-Cig, CNR, Cerdia); (ii) destroy CA to cellulose/paper (UnB/Poiato, BuffIndia reported, Code Effort paper); (iii) use butts as filler and encapsulate (Keenat patent, TerraCycle lumber, construction materials). They have different exposure pathways and cannot be ranked on one "cleanliness" number.
2. **The toxic fraction needs a sink.** Every route produces a concentrate: wash water, dark liquor, solvent/water, off-gas, sludge. No operator in this batch publishes quantitative data for that stream. A route whose concentrate has no lawful disposal route is incomplete regardless of product purity.
3. **Closed-loop claims need boundaries.** MéGo! and TchaoMegot claim closed loops; Re-Cig openly uses an external treatment partner. At least one of these claims is likely loose about purge/bleed. This is a discriminating question, not an accusation.
4. **Mass balances are mostly absent.** Only Re-Cig (~39 % polymer) and MéGo! (80/12/8 company split) publish anything usable, and neither is a dry-mass balance.
5. **Certification language ≠ assay.** "ISO", "End of Waste", "REACh", "INERIS", "medical-grade" are process/administrative signals. They do not state which analytes were measured at what limit in the product.
