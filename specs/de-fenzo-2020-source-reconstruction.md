# Source reconstruction: De Fenzo et al., water/ethanol recovery of cellulose acetate

Date read: 2026-11-02. Stage: desk reconstruction; independent review pending. Not a laboratory SOP or a claim of product safety.

## Plain-language mechanism
The reported cleaning route washes contaminants out while retaining cellulose-acetate material. Acetone is used later to make a demonstration film from recovered polymer. Do not describe that film-making step as the primary cleaning solvent. These operations have different purposes and outputs.

## What was actually evaluated
- Section 2 reports analysis on five untreated and five recovered samples. Do not treat those counts as a large independent industrial reproducibility study.
- Optical/SEM imaging examines fiber morphology; recovered fibers are described as having irregular surfaces and reduced volume.
- FTIR compares polymer functional groups; DSC/TGA and kinetic analyses examine thermal behavior. These support material-characterization statements, not complete removal of all toxicants.
- Section 3.1 reports flame atomic absorption results, an instrument detection limit below 0.01 mg/L, and 0.04 ppm titanium. The preparation/basis and analyte-specific reporting are insufficient here to turn that instrument concentration limit into a universal mg/kg product limit. EDX percentages and the atomic-absorption result are different measurement contexts; do not equate them.
- The paper supports a polymer-recovery research lead and a film demonstration. It does not establish a certified consumer product, a universal contaminant-removal percentage, or a closed solvent-recovery plant.

## Important limits and unknowns
1. Feedstock mass, wet/dry basis, water/ethanol loading, per-wash contact times, agitation, and waste-treatment details need resolution.
2. No complete batch mass balance or defensible per-tonne yield/cost is extracted from this paper. Do not use the inherited fixed butt-composition split as experimental input.
3. No validated multi-contaminant clearance claim follows from FTIR/TGA resemblance, lack of odor, or a transparent film. Nicotine, relevant PAHs/TSNAs, metals, solvent residues, and product-specific emissions/leaching require appropriate analytical endpoints.
4. Brittleness is an observed product limitation. Plasticizer selection is described as ongoing work, not a proven general repair of polymer quality.
5. Sterilization is not chemical detoxification. The procedure is not fully specified.
6. The Funding section states "No Funding" while acknowledgments thank Essequadro for funding/support. Preserve this reporting tension rather than simplifying it to unambiguously unfunded research.

## Engineering interpretation and next questions
Hypothesis, not source result: fiber-preserving washing could be a baseline for comparing water/solvent extraction with polymer dissolution routes. A closed-loop design would need solvent recovery, recycle-impurity control, treated-water accounting, vapor containment, drying safety, and a legitimate residue-disposal route. None is established by a neat-looking sample.

Suggested next desk work: independently extract the exact analytical scope; obtain missing wash loadings or author/supplementary information if available; define a qualified-lab contaminant validation plan; compare time, polymer integrity, and waste pathways against the Chinese dissolution route. No invented simulation or real experiment was performed for this note.
