## Disclosed in P's accessed description
Prepare fibers from butts, then contact them with dense pressurized fluid, preferably supercritical, in a chamber/autoclave. The fluid acts as a solvent, particularly for organic contaminants; CO2 is preferred, not the sole listed fluid.

Nested general pressure envelopes: 0-2000 bar, preferably 50-350, preferably 280-320. General temperatures: 0-400 C, preferably 15-80. These are disclosure envelopes, not uniformly feasible/supercritical CO2 states or validated optima. The CO2-specific passage states at least about 31 C and 73.85 bar; conditions below these cannot be called supercritical solely because a generic range includes them. Dense/subcritical variants are also mentioned.

Potential uses include textiles/padding, insulation, filtering and plastics. Listed uses are not verified approvals/sales. Purification requirements vary with application according to P itself.

## Company statement, separately attributed
C describes removing tobacco/paper and using a neutral natural solvent recycled in a continuous closed loop. It says no water/no TOXIC solvent, not absence of a solvent. Its accessed outline does not name the solvent chemistry; a CO2 patent embodiment does not alone prove the current commercial recipe.

C reports 99.7% insulating fibers/0.3% concentrate and separately 30 L fiber generating 100 mL effluent. No common dry-mass basis or analyte-specific removal is established here. Do not reinterpret as 99.7% purity, a one-tonne balance, or necessarily dry powder. C claims laboratory/REACh-related validation; obtain the reports and end-use scope before relying on it. Do not assume separated tobacco/paper is safe compost.

Separation, recycle-quality control and residue handling are requirements to investigate, not a verified bill of materials from the accessible excerpt. Closed-loop does not imply zero makeup, 100% recovery, no contaminated filters/sorbents or no waste treatment.
