# Recycling cigarette butts

Butt Lab is a public research notebook for curious readers and technical reviewers. It uses papers, patents and company descriptions to explain how cigarette butts might be recycled. It also offers AI hypotheses where the evidence runs out. The project has not performed any physical experiments.

## What a cigarette butt actually is

Most of the filter is **cellulose acetate**, a plastic made into fine fibres. A spent butt can also contain paper, leftover tobacco and ash. A recycling route can separate those materials, recover the polymer, change it into something else or use it as part of a new material. It can also transfer contaminants without removing the hazard. A cleaner-looking filter or a useful-looking product is not enough to tell the difference.

## How the evidence is labelled

- **Reported:** A source describes or measures something. Check the kind of source before drawing a conclusion.
- **AI inference:** Reasoning based on the sources, rather than a statement from the source itself.
- **AI hypothesis:** A possible explanation or candidate design that has not been validated in this project.
- **Unknown:** The available evidence did not settle the question. A gap is not filled just by proposing an explanation.

These labels describe findings and ideas. A bibliography or glossary entry is material you can consult, not an evidence claim by itself.

## What should a reviewer check?

Follow the recovered material, solid rejects, liquids and air emissions. Ask where each contaminant went, how it was measured, and whether each output has an appropriate destination. Cadmium, lead, arsenic and mercury cannot be averaged away: an uncharacterised stream remains unresolved. Product performance and suitability for a specific use need separate evidence, even when all the incoming mass has been accounted for.

See [how the research was done](/research-method/) to propose a correction.
